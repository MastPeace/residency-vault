## How to read this site

Every country page has a header with key facts, a monitoring panel, the conditions of each program, and a Country Snapshot table with sourced numbers. This glossary explains the columns, labels and abbreviations you will meet there.

## Country list columns

Click a column title to sort; click the * next to a title for a short explanation. Hover a value to see where it came from.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Region | Region code; hover for the full name (see **Region codes**). |
| Tags | Editorial #tags (see **Fit tags**). |
| Programs | Icons for the residency routes the country offers (see **Program types**). Routes that are closed or not available are left out. The program buttons above the table filter countries that offer all selected routes. |
| Citizen yrs | Years of legal residence before you can apply for citizenship under the standard route. `10+ yr` means ten years or more; `no path` means there is no realistic route for a foreigner. |
| Visa-free | Passport strength: the number of destinations a holder of that country's passport can enter visa-free (a Henley-type visa-free count). It describes the passport you would get after naturalisation, not the one you hold now. |
| Cost idx | Numbeo cost-of-living index with New York City = 100; lower means cheaper. Ranges on the page are shown as their midpoint. |
| Rent $/mo | Typical monthly rent of a 1-bedroom apartment, usually in the city centre, converted to US dollars at approximate fixed exchange rates (a range stays a range). It is a rough comparison figure, not a quote. |
| Invest min | The cheapest qualifying amount of the country's investment-residency route (Golden Visa style: property, fund, business or deposit), in US dollars. Amounts stated in other currencies are converted at approximate fixed rates and marked `≈` (rounded to two significant digits); hover to see the original amount and the source quote. Empty means the route is closed, absent or has no stated amount. |
| Nomad visa | Whether a digital-nomad or remote-work residence exists, with the headline income requirement per month or year, in US dollars (converted and marked `≈` when the page uses another currency). `No` means the page says there is none; empty means the page does not say. |
| Tax % | Effective personal income tax range for a remote worker, in percent. Indicative only: real tax depends on residency days, regime and income. |
| Trust | Confidence level of the page: how well it is backed by sources (see **Confidence** below). 3 bars = high, 2 = medium, 1 = low. |

Invest min, Nomad visa and Tax % are extracted from the country pages into `data/facts.json`; every value keeps the quote it came from and is dropped if the quote cannot be found on the page.

## Confidence

- **High**: key facts are confirmed by official or authoritative sources.
- **Medium**: a mix of official sources and secondary or estimated data.
- **Low**: mostly unverified, or important data is missing. Check the sources before relying on it.

## Fit tags

Tags are editorial and help you filter the list. They are not official classifications.

- **#passport** (Strong passport): citizenship would give a high visa-free count.
- **#it** (IT-friendly): a remote-work or tech-friendly route plus a workable environment for IT specialists.
- **#path** (Clear pathway): a well-defined, documented route from residence to a passport.
- **#ru** (Popular with RU): a common destination for Russian-speaking applicants.
- **#yacht** (Yacht captain): a country with a maritime or yachting infrastructure relevant to captains.

## Source marks

- **✓**: the value comes from an official or authoritative source.
- **~**: an estimate or a secondary source; the value may be outdated.
- **No reliable data / No recent data**: nothing credible was found. The field stays empty on purpose rather than guessed.

## Program types

Each type has a comparison page across countries.

- 💰 [Investment residency](concepts/investment-residency.html): residence in exchange for an investment, often called a Golden Visa.
- 🛂 [Citizenship by investment](concepts/citizenship-by-investment.html): a passport in exchange for a contribution or investment (CBI).
- 🏦 [Passive-income residency](concepts/passive-income-residency.html): residence based on stable income that does not require local work.
- 🌴 [Retired residency](concepts/retired-residency.html): residence for pensioners with a foreign pension.
- 💻 [Digital nomad visa](concepts/digital-nomad-visa.html): residence for people who work remotely for foreign clients or employers.
- 💼 [Employment residency](concepts/employment-residency.html): residence tied to a local job offer.
- 🧠 [Highly skilled employment](concepts/highly-skilled-employment.html): fast-track permits for qualified specialists, including the EU Blue Card.
- 🚀 [Startup / entrepreneur](concepts/startup-entrepreneur.html): residence for founders and business owners.
- 🎓 [Student residency](concepts/student-residency.html): residence for enrolled students.
- 👨‍👩‍👧 [Family reunification](concepts/family-reunification.html): residence for relatives of a legal resident or citizen.

## Abbreviations

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Golden Visa (GV) | Investment residency programme. |
| CBI | Citizenship by investment. |
| DN / Digital Nomad Visa | Residence permit for remote workers. |
| FIP | Financially Independent Person, a passive-income route (for example in Greece). |
| D7 / D8 | Portuguese residence visas: D7 for passive income and retirees, D8 for digital nomads. |
| Non-Lucrative | Spanish residence for people living on savings or passive income without working locally. |
| Blue Card | EU permit for highly qualified workers. |
| Schengen | The EU zone without internal border checks; a short-stay visa works in all member states. |
| EU / EEA | European Union / European Economic Area; citizens move and work freely across them. |
| non-dom | Special tax regime where foreign-source income is taxed lightly or not at all. |
| PPP | Purchasing power parity. |
| FATF | Financial Action Task Force; its grey and black lists flag countries with weak anti-money-laundering controls and can slow bank transfers. |
| CRS | Common Reporting Standard: automatic exchange of bank-account information between tax authorities. |
| YoY | Year over year. |
| RU | Russian citizens or the Russian-speaking audience. |
