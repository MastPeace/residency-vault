## Definition

Employment residency — standard work permits tied to a job offer from a local employer, covering the broadest category of economic migration. Unlike highly skilled employment ([[highly-skilled-employment]]), this typically has no elevated qualification or salary thresholds — the core requirement is a binding job offer. Key friction points: labor market tests (employer must prove no suitable local/EU candidate), annual quotas (Italy's Decreto Flussi, UK visa caps), and employer lock-in (visa tied to sponsoring employer for a period). Almost every country offers this pathway and it is the most common route to permanent residency and citizenship for ordinary workers.

## Current State

84 countries in the wiki have employment residency programs — essentially every country with a structured immigration system. Quality varies dramatically: EU Blue Card countries have a parallel high-skill track; GCC countries tie visas to employers with limited portability; some countries apply strict labor market tests while others are relatively open. This category is the baseline work immigration route; virtually every country offers it, so the comparison focuses on notable structural differences.
