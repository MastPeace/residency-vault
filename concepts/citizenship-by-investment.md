## Definition

Citizenship by investment (CBI) — direct acquisition of citizenship (and passport) in exchange for a qualifying investment, with no prior residence requirement. This is fundamentally different from investment residency ([[investment-residency]]): CBI grants citizenship immediately (or within months), while investment residency grants only a residence permit at first. The market is small: only ~5 countries globally operate active, well-established CBI programs (Antigua & Barbuda, St. Kitts & Nevis, Grenada, Turkey, Egypt). Jordan has a program but uptake is limited. Several programs have closed: Montenegro (Dec 2022), Malta (formerly MEIN — struck down by ECJ Apr 2025). Argentina has legislated CBI (Decree 524/2025) but it is NOT yet operational. Ghana is pending legislation.

Caribbean CBI programs (Antigua, St. Kitts, Grenada) offer visa-free access to ~140-150 destinations, including the Schengen Area and UK, with donation options starting at $230K-$250K. Turkey's CBI provides a G20 passport with US E-2 eligibility and ~110 visa-free destinations from $400K real estate. Processing is fast: 3-4 months for Turkey, 3-6 months for Caribbean programs. The industry faces ongoing pressure from the EU and OECD over security and tax concerns.

## Current State

Only 5 active, operational CBI programs worldwide. This is a deliberately small market — most countries prohibit direct sale of citizenship. The Caribbean quartet (Antigua, St. Kitts, Grenada, Dominica — though Dominica is not in the wiki) + Turkey + Egypt. Several "near-CBI" programs (Malta exceptional naturalization — terminated; Montenegro — closed; Argentina — legislated but not operational; Ghana — pending) highlight the contested nature of this pathway.

*Not available* in the vast majority of countries. Most EU states, US, Canada, Australia, UK, and 90+ others explicitly prohibit or do not offer CBI. The handful of programs that exist face ongoing regulatory pressure from the EU and OECD.

## Key Trends
- **EU squeeze**: Malta MEIN struck down by ECJ (Apr 2025); EU pushing Caribbean programs to raise minimums and tighten vetting
- **Price escalation**: Caribbean donation minimums rose from $100K to $230-250K range over 2022–2024
- **Fraud crackdown**: Turkey revoked 6,134 fraudulently obtained citizenships (Aug 2026)
- **No new entrants**: The market is shrinking, not growing — Argentina and Ghana are the only pending programs, both uncertain
