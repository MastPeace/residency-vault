## Overview
Belgium is an EU/Schengen member hosting EU institutions (Brussels). Its immigration is managed by the Immigration Office (Office des étrangers / Dienst Vreemdelingenzaken). Belgium offers a professional card for self-employed non-EU nationals and standard employment routes. No dedicated golden visa or digital nomad visa exists. Permanent residency after 5 years of uninterrupted legal residence; citizenship after 5 years. ^[https://dofi.ibz.be/en]

## Per-Program Detail

### Highly Skilled Employment
- **EU Blue Card / Single Permit** — for non-EU nationals with a job offer from a Belgian employer
- **Conditions**: higher education degree (3+ years); employment contract ≥1 year; salary ≥1.5× average gross annual salary (~€50,000 in 2026). For shortage occupations: 1.2× average. ^[https://dofi.ibz.be/en]
- **Duration**: contract length + 3 months, max 4 years; renewable
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years legal residence; language (French/Dutch/German); integration course
- **Official source**: ^[https://dofi.ibz.be/en/themes/professional/working/blue-card]
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (language requirement + complex regional bureaucracy); sourced from expat forums

### Startup Entrepreneur
- **Professional Card** *(Carte professionnelle / Beroepskaart)* — for self-employed non-EU nationals
- **Conditions**: detailed business plan (max 20 pages); proof of economic benefit to Belgium; clean criminal record; professional qualifications relevant to the activity; sufficient financial means. Application submitted from country of residence via Belgian embassy. ^[https://info.hub.brussels/en/guide/permits-regulations-and-obligations/professional-card-non-european-nationals]
- **Duration**: 1–5 years initially, renewable
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years legal residence
- **Official source**: ^[https://info.hub.brussels/en/guide/permits-regulations-and-obligations/professional-card-non-european-nationals]
- **Community verdict**: Works well (established pathway, reliable process); moderate bureaucratic complexity

### Employment Residency
- **Single Permit** — combined work + residence authorisation for employees
- **Conditions**: job offer from Belgian employer; employer applies in Belgium; labour market test unless shortage occupation; salary ≥ applicable minimum wage (varies by sector/region) ^[https://dofi.ibz.be/en]
- **Duration**: tied to contract length
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years legal residence
- **Official source**: ^[https://dofi.ibz.be/en/themes/professional/working/single-permit]
- **Community verdict**: Works well for qualified professionals; processing times subject to regional variation

### Student Residency
- **Student visa (D-visa)** — for non-EU students enrolled in Belgian institutions
- **Conditions**: acceptance letter from recognised institution; proof of sufficient means (~€27,100/year blocked account or scholarship in 2026); health insurance; good conduct certificate ^[https://canada.diplomatie.belgium.be/en/visa/visa-belgium/long-stay-visa-d-visa]
- **Duration**: duration of study programme + 1 job-search year
- **Path to citizenship**: stays on student permit count at 50% for citizenship calculation
- **Official source**: ^[https://canada.diplomatie.belgium.be/en/visa/visa-belgium/long-stay-visa-d-visa]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; moderate tuition (€835–4,000/year non-EEA)

### Family Reunification
- **Family reunification** — for nuclear family members of legal residents
- **Conditions**: sponsor must hold valid permit ≥1 year; stable income ≥120% of social integration income (~€1,570/mo); adequate housing; family member applies at Belgian embassy abroad ^[https://dofi.ibz.be/en/themes/family]
- **Duration**: tied to sponsor's permit
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years legal residence (independent application possible after 5 years)
- **Official source**: ^[https://dofi.ibz.be/en/themes/family/family-reunification]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; income threshold may be high for some

## Citizenship
- **Standard**: 5 years of uninterrupted legal residence
- **Language**: proficiency in Dutch, French, or German (at least A2)
- **Integration**: proof of social and economic integration (employment, education, or sufficient means)
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted since 2008
- **Application**: via commune; fee ~€150; processing up to 5 months
- **EU passport**: yes ^[https://www.brusselstimes.com/2235048/how-to-apply-for-permanent-residency-in-belgium-tbtb]

## Tax
- **Progressive income tax**: 25%–50% (plus municipal surcharge ~7%).
- **Expat tax regime** *(new from 2025)*: non-resident taxation treatment; 30% of salary tax-free (capped at €90,000); employer-paid expenses (housing, school fees) excluded from taxable income; minimum salary €75,000 (or €57,000 for researchers). Replaced previous expat regime. ^[https://www.brusselstimes.be/1957907/belgium-relaxes-tax-regime-for-expats-what-changes]
- **Corporate tax**: 25% (reduced 20% for SMEs on first €100K).
- **No wealth tax**; capital gains on shares generally tax-free for private investors.
- **Municipal tax**: varies by commune (~0–9% surcharge on income tax).

## IT / Digital Nomad
Belgium has no dedicated [[digital-nomad]] visa. IT professionals typically use:
- EU Blue Card / Single Permit (employer-sponsored)
- Professional Card (freelance/self-employed IT consultants)
- Belgium hosts major EU tech policy bodies but its tech startup scene is smaller than NL/DE. Brussels, Antwerp, and Ghent are main hubs.

## Yacht / Maritime
Belgium has a short North Sea coastline. Major port: Antwerp (Europe's second-largest). Yacht registration under Belgian flag is available but not a major yachting hub. Ostend and Nieuwpoort are the main marinas.

## Notes for RU citizens
Belgium applies EU sanctions against Russia. Visa processing for Russian citizens faces delays and enhanced scrutiny. The professional card route is open in principle. No specific ban on Russian nationals, but banking (opening accounts, processing payments) may be difficult. Belgian consulates in Russia operate at reduced capacity — applications often routed through third-country consulates.
