## Overview
Costa Rica's immigration is managed by the Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería (DGME). The system offers several well-defined residency categories: **Pensionado** (retirees), **Rentista** (passive income), **Inversionista** (investors), and a **Digital Nomad Visa** (Law No. 10008, 1+1 years). Costa Rica is a popular expat/retirement destination. The Costa Rican passport provides ~150 visa-free destinations. Citizenship requires 5 years (Ibero-American/Spanish by birth), 7 years (others by birth), or 2 years (spouses of Costa Ricans). Costa Rica has no military (since 1948) and consistently ranks high on the Happy Planet Index. ^[https://www.migracion.go.cr]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Inversionista
- **Conditions**: Investment ≥ USD 200,000 in one of: real estate, active business, reforestation project, or other qualifying investment registered in Costa Rica. ^[https://www.twoweeksincostarica.com/digital-nomad-visa]
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years temporary, renewable. Permanent residency after 3 years of temporary.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years (Ibero-American/Spanish by birth) or 7 years (others) from first legal residency.
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.go.cr
- **Community verdict**: Works well (well-established category; USD 200,000 threshold has been stable).

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Rentista
- **Conditions**: Guaranteed income ≥ USD 2,500/mo for at least 2 years, certified by a bank. Alternative: USD 60,000 deposit in an approved Costa Rican bank. Covers spouse and children under 25. ^[https://bgacorp.com/costa-rica-rentista-visa-requirements]
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable. Permanent residency after 3 years.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years (Ibero-American/Spanish) or 7 years (others).
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.go.cr
- **Community verdict**: Works well (but income threshold is high for Latin America; Caja/CCSS contributions add significant cost).

### digital_nomad_visa
- **Official name**: Estancia para Trabajadores y Prestadores Remotos de Servicios (Law No. 10008)
- **Conditions**: Remote work for foreign employer/clients. Income ≥ USD 3,000/mo (individual) or USD 4,000/mo (with dependents). Valid health insurance covering stay. One-time ~USD 100 application fee. Income tax exemption on foreign earnings. ^[https://www.visitcostarica.com/en/costa-rica/digital-nomads]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable once (2 years max). Does NOT count toward permanent residency clock.
- **Path to citizenship**: No direct path. Must switch to Rentista/Pensionado/Inversionista to start the residency clock.
- **Official source**: https://www.visitcostarica.com/en/costa-rica/digital-nomads
- **Community verdict**: Works well for lifestyle (tax-free foreign income, duty-free equipment import, bank accounts, driver's license recognition). Dead-end for long-term residency.

### highly_skilled_employment
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Trabajo
- **Conditions**: Employment contract with Costa Rican employer + work permit (permiso de trabajo). DGME approval required.
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5–7 years.
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.go.cr
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (Costa Rican labor market is small; work permits prioritize locals).

### startup_entrepreneur
Subsumed under Inversionista. Business investment ≥ USD 200,000.

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Trabajo
- **Conditions**: Binding job offer from Costa Rican employer. Employer must demonstrate no local candidate available for the position.
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5–7 years.
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.go.cr
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (high bar for work permits).

### student_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Estudiante
- **Conditions**: Enrollment in Costa Rican institution.
- **Duration / renewal**: Course duration.
- **Path to citizenship**: Student time counts.
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.go.cr
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: Residencia — Vínculo Familiar
- **Conditions**: First-degree family tie: spouse, child, or parent of a Costa Rican citizen or permanent resident. Marriage-based: 2-year residency + 2-year marriage for citizenship. ^[https://legalclarity.org/costa-rica-dual-citizenship-requirements-and-process]
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable → permanent.
- **Path to citizenship**: 2 years (spouse of Costa Rican).
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.go.cr
- **Community verdict**: Works well (fastest citizenship track).

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Pensionado
- **Conditions**: Lifetime pension ≥ USD 1,000/mo from abroad. The most popular residency category for expat retirees. ^[https://www.twoweeksincostarica.com/digital-nomad-visa]
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable. Permanent after 3 years.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5–7 years.
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.go.cr
- **Community verdict**: Works well (most established retiree visa in Central America; Caja contributions required — can be costly).

### citizenship_by_investment
Not available.

## Citizenship
- **Naturalization (Articles 14–15, Constitution)**: ^[https://legalclarity.org/costa-rica-dual-citizenship-requirements-and-process]
  - 5 years of legal residency: Central Americans, Spanish, Ibero-Americans by birth.
  - 7 years: all other nationalities (including naturalized Ibero-Americans).
  - 2 years: spouses of Costa Rican citizens (2 years marriage + 2 years physical presence).
- **Time counted**: Both temporary and permanent residency count toward the qualifying period.
- **Language/civics**: Spanish proficiency exam + Costa Rican history and values exam (minimum grade 70). Exempt if completed school in Costa Rica.
- **Processing**: ~10 months after document delivery to TSE (Supreme Electoral Tribunal).
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted since 1995 (Law No. 7514). Naturalization petition includes formal renunciation language, but Costa Rica does NOT enforce loss of prior nationality.
- **Passport**: ~150 visa-free destinations.

## Tax
- **Tax system**: Territorial. Costa Rica taxes only Costa Rican-source income. Foreign-source income not taxed. ^[https://www.taxesforexpats.com/country-guides/costa-rica//costa-rica-digital-nomad-visa.html]
- **Residency**: Tax residency generally triggered by 183+ days.
- **Digital nomads**: Foreign income explicitly tax-exempt under the DN visa regime.
- **Caja (CCSS)**: Mandatory socialized healthcare + pension contributions for legal residents — this is a significant ongoing cost (~7–12% of declared income). Many applicants underestimate this.

## IT / Digital Nomad
- Well-established digital nomad visa with tax-free foreign income, duty-free equipment import, bank account access, and foreign driver's license recognition.
- Major limitation: DN time does NOT count toward permanent residency or citizenship.
- Internet: generally good in Central Valley (San José, Escazú, Santa Ana), Pacific coast (Tamarindo) spotty. Coworking scene strong in San José and beach towns.
- Pro: close to US time zones; stable democracy; no military.

## Yacht / Maritime
Pacific and Caribbean coastlines make Costa Rica a notable yachting destination in Central America. Marinas at Los Sueños, Marina Pez Vela, Golfito. No special yacht/captain residency program, but Pensionado/Rentista are accessible to yacht owners.

## Notes for RU citizens
- Russian citizens: visa-free for tourism up to 90 days (Group 1 per DGME classification).
- Residency applications: documents must be apostilled, translated into Spanish by official translator.
- Banking: possible with DIMEX (residency card); Caja registration required.
- Costa Rica is popular among RU citizens for the territorial tax system and pensionado route. No specific restrictions on Russian applicants.
- Wait times: residency processing can be very slow (12–15 months for Rentista/Pensionado).

## Community pulse
- **Overall**: Costa Rica is a top expat destination for good reason — stable democracy, territorial tax, quality healthcare, and a clear residency framework. The trade-off: high costs (Caja, high income thresholds vs. regional peers) and slow bureaucracy.
- **Processing**: Residency approvals take 12–15 months on average. DGME is under-resourced; delays are common.
- **Rejection risk**: Low if documents are properly apostilled and translated. Incomplete paperwork causes the longest delays.
- **Cost**: Residency application fees ~USD 50; Caja contributions are the real ongoing cost.

## Country Card

### General description
Costa Rica is a small Central American country between the Pacific and Caribbean, known for its political stability, biodiversity, and "pura vida" lifestyle. It is the only country in the world without a standing military (abolished 1948). Costa Rica offers a well-structured residency system: the legendary Pensionado visa ($1,000/mo — one of the world's best-known retiree programs), Rentista ($2,500/mo), Inversionista ($200,000), and a Digital Nomad Visa ($3,000/mo, 1+1 year). Citizenship takes 5 years (Ibero-Americans/Spanish) or 7 years (others). Costa Rica has a territorial tax system (0% foreign income) and consistently ranks among the region's best in rule of law and freedom.

### Well-known strong points
- No military since 1948 — long history of peace, stability, and democratic governance
- Territorial tax system: 0% tax on foreign-source income
- Legendary Pensionado visa: $1,000/mo lifetime pension → residency, with extensive senior discounts
- High rule of law (WJP 0.70) and freedom (FH 91/100) — top tier in Latin America
- Exceptional biodiversity and eco-tourism — 25% of territory is protected
- Strong passport: 149 visa-free destinations, Schengen access

### Well-known problems
- Highest cost of living in Central America (Numbeo 47.5) — comparable to southern Europe
- Digital Nomad visa income thresholds are high ($3,000/mo solo, $4,000 family) and DN time does NOT count toward residency
- Caja (CCSS) mandatory contributions: ~7–12% of declared income — a significant hidden cost for residents
- Bureaucracy is slow: residency approvals take 12–15 months on average
- Long citizenship timeline for non-Ibero-Americans: 7 years
- Internet and infrastructure quality variable outside Central Valley and tourist zones
