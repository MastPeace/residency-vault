## Overview
Chile's immigration is managed by the Servicio Nacional de Migraciones (SERMIG) under Law 21.325 (2022). The system runs on four statuses: permanencia transitoria (tourist, 90+90 days), residencia temporal (16 subcategories, up to 2 years), residencia definitiva (permanent), and citizenship. Chile has **no dedicated digital nomad visa** — remote workers use the Rentista visa or tourist extensions. The Chilean passport ranks 13th globally (~175 visa-free destinations) and is the only Latin American passport with US ESTA access. ^[https://serviciomigraciones.cl]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Inversionista (Decree 177/2022, subcategory under Law 21.325)
- **Conditions**: Qualifying productive investment of approximately USD 500,000. Business plan, local registration, and evidence of funds required. Real estate alone may not qualify without business activity. ^[https://goldenharbors.com/articles/chile-citizenship]
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years temporary, renewable. Can apply for Residencia Definitiva after 2 years.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years total legal residence (including temporary + permanent time) from Estampado Electrónico date.
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (high threshold; processing times 6–8 months for temporary, ~18 months for permanent).

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Rentista
- **Conditions**: Recurring passive income from abroad: pensions, rental income, dividends, annuities. Freelance/remote-work income does NOT qualify. No published fixed minimum; ~USD 1,000–1,500/mo is the practical expectation. Income must be documented with bank statements and translated. ^[https://www.expat.cl/residency/temporary-residency-chile]
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable in 2-year increments.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years from first Estampado Electrónico.
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Works well (viable for retirees and investors with documented passive income).

### digital_nomad_visa
- **Official name**: Not available. Chile has no digital nomad visa or remote-work visa.
- **Conditions**: Remote workers may enter as tourists (90 days, extendable to 180) and work for foreign employers de facto, or apply for Rentista (if income qualifies as passive) or a work visa (if employed by a Chilean entity). ^[https://www.expat.cl/residency/chile-digital-nomad-remote-work]
- **Duration / renewal**: Tourist: 90 + 90 days. Rentista: 2 yr.
- **Path to citizenship**: Tourist time does NOT count. Rentista → 5 yr clock.
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (no clear legal framework for remote workers; Rentista excludes active freelance income).

### highly_skilled_employment
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Trabajo (Subordinado)
- **Conditions**: Employment contract or formal job offer from a Chilean employer. Contract must be registered. Self-employed professionals may use a services contract. ^[https://serviciomigraciones.cl/en/categoria/temporary-residence/]
- **Duration / renewal**: Up to 2 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years from Estampado Electrónico.
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Works well (standard employment route; employer sponsorship required).

### startup_entrepreneur
Subsumed under the Inversionista category. Business plan + qualifying productive investment required.

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Trabajo (Subordinado)
- **Conditions**: Binding employment contract with a Chilean employer. For foreign nationals performing remunerated activities as employees or with a formal job offer (contract must be submitted within 45 days of arrival). ^[https://www.fragomen.com/insights/chile-new-regulation-restructures-visa-categories.html]
- **Duration / renewal**: Up to 2 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years from Estampado Electrónico.
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### student_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Estudiante
- **Conditions**: Enrollment in a state-recognized Chilean educational institution. Full-time study required.
- **Duration / renewal**: Course duration, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Student time counts toward the 5-year residence requirement.
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Reunificación Familiar
- **Conditions**: Certified family bond (spouse, child, parent) with a Chilean citizen or permanent resident. Sponsor must demonstrate economic solvency.
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years from Estampado Electrónico (or 2 years under Art. 85 qualified pathway for spouses of Chileans).
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Residencia Temporal — Jubilado / Pensionado
- **Conditions**: Demonstrable pension from abroad. No published fixed minimum by SERMIG; ~USD 1,000/mo is the practical expectation. Pension documentation must be apostilled and translated.
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years from Estampado Electrónico.
- **Official source**: https://serviciomigraciones.cl
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### citizenship_by_investment
Not available. No direct citizenship-by-investment program exists. The Investor visa provides a residency path → citizenship after 5 years.

## Citizenship
- **Standard naturalization**: 5 years of continuous legal residence counted from the first Estampado Electrónico (temporary residency stamp). Applicant must hold active Residencia Definitiva at filing. ^[https://goldenharbors.com/articles/chile-citizenship-by-naturalization]
- **Qualified naturalization (Art. 85, Law 21.325)**: 2 years for spouses of Chileans (marriage ≥2 years registered in Chile), close relatives of Chileans, or adoptees.
- **Language**: Spanish proficiency required; no formal exam yet but a civics/culture exam is planned per 2024 policy announcements.
- **Processing time**: 2–3 years from filing to Decreto Exento.
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted since 2005 (Law 20.050). No renunciation required.
- **Passport**: ~175 visa-free destinations (Henley 2026), US ESTA eligible.

## Tax
- **Residency trigger**: 183 days in Chile in a calendar year, or center of vital interests.
- **Tax system**: Worldwide income taxation. Progressive rates 0%–40% (Impuesto Global Complementario). ^[https://studiopanamaitalia.com/en/residenze-e-cittadinanze/residenza-in-cile]
- **Corporate tax**: 27% (First Category Tax). Foreign tax credits available.
- **No territorial option** — Chile taxes worldwide income of residents.

## IT / Digital Nomad
- No digital nomad visa. Remote workers rely on: (a) tourist entry (90 days, extendable to 180) — works de facto but no legal work rights; (b) Rentista visa — requires genuine passive income, active freelance excluded; (c) Work visa — requires Chilean employer.
- Internet: excellent infrastructure, among the best in Latin America. Santiago and Valparaíso have strong coworking scenes.

## Yacht / Maritime
Not a major yachting hub. Standard foreign-flag vessel rules apply. No special yacht/captain regime comparable to Panama or Malta.

## Notes for RU citizens
- Russian citizens require a tourist visa from a Chilean consulate. RU passport holders are NOT visa-exempt.
- Residency applications require apostilled documents translated into Spanish.
- No specific bilateral restrictions targeting Russian applicants.
- Banking access: once you have a RUT (tax ID) and cédula, major banks (Banco de Chile, Santander, BCI) open accounts; compliance checks are standard for RU nationals post-2022.

## Community pulse
- **Processing times**: Temporary residence ~6–8 months, permanent ~18 months, citizenship 2–3 years. These are observed averages, not guarantees.
- **Rejection risk**: Moderate if documentation is incomplete. SERMIG issues "additional requirements" frequently; silence closes the file.
- **Overall**: Chile is a mid-difficulty destination. Strong passport reward (ESTA, ~175 visa-free) justifies the 5-year wait.

## Status
- Last change: Law 21.325 (2022) restructured all visa categories into 16 subcategories under Residencia Temporal.
- Planned: civics/culture exam for naturalization (announced 2024, not yet implemented).
- Last monitored: 2026-09-14.

## Country Card

### General description
Chile is a long, narrow country on South America's Pacific coast, stretching from the Atacama Desert to Patagonia. It has the highest GDP per capita in Latin America (~$32,000 PPP) and is the only Latin American country with US ESTA access. Chile's immigration system under Law 21.325 (2022) offers a Rentista visa (~$1,000–1,500/mo passive income), an Investor visa (~$500,000), and work visas — but no dedicated digital nomad visa. The Chilean passport is the strongest in Latin America (176 visa-free, rank 14). Citizenship requires 5 years from the first residency stamp.

### Well-known strong points
- Strongest passport in Latin America: 176 visa-free destinations, US ESTA eligible
- Highest GDP per capita in the region and strong institutions (WJP 0.66, FH 94/100)
- Political stability and reliable rule of law — best in Latin America alongside Uruguay and Costa Rica
- OECD member; modern infrastructure, healthcare, and banking
- Geographic diversity: Atacama Desert, Lake District, Patagonia, wine regions
- Moderate cost of living relative to developed-world quality of life

### Well-known problems
- No digital nomad visa — remote workers lack a clear legal framework (Rentista excludes active freelance income)
- Long processing times: temporary residence 6–8 months, permanent ~18 months, citizenship 2–3 years
- High inequality and cost of living relative to local salaries (Numbeo index 40.5)
- Worldwide taxation with no territorial option — 0%–40% progressive
- Spanish-only bureaucracy; no English-language government services
- Investor visa threshold is high (~$500,000) compared to regional peers like Paraguay ($70,000)
