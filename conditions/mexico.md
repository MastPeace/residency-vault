## Overview
Mexico's immigration system is managed by the Instituto Nacional de Migración (INM). Mexico offers Temporary Residency (1–4 years) and Permanent Residency through economic solvency, family ties, or work. There is no dedicated digital nomad visa, but Temporary Residency covers remote workers. Mexico has one of the largest expat communities in Latin America. Citizenship is available after 5 years of legal residence (2 years for spouses/parents of Mexicans). ^[https://www.gob.mx/inm]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
Mexico does not operate a formal Golden Visa or investment residency program. The economic solvency route (savings ≥ ~$74,000 USD for Temporary, ≥ ~$294,000 for Permanent) is the closest equivalent. Foreigners may own real estate in Mexico (with restrictions in the restricted zone — fideicomiso required within 50 km of coast / 100 km of border).

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Residente Temporal por Solvencia Económica
- **Conditions**: Monthly income ≥ ~$4,400–$4,600 USD for 6 months OR savings/investments ≥ ~$68,000–$80,000 USD for 12 months. Exact amount varies by consulate and exchange rate. Washington consulate 2026: MXN $83,640/mo income or MXN $1,394,000 in savings over 12 months. ^[https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/washington/images/2026/visas/eng/Temporary_resident.pdf]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year initial, renewable annually up to 4 years total. After 4 years → eligible for permanent residency.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years legal residence (temporary + permanent). 18 months physical presence in last 2 years required at application.
- **Official source**: https://www.gob.mx/inm
- **Community verdict**: Works well (consulate discretion creates variance in financial thresholds; well-trodden path).

### digital_nomad_visa
Mexico does not offer a dedicated digital nomad visa. Remote workers typically enter as tourists (up to 180 days) or apply for Temporary Residency via economic solvency. Working remotely for foreign employers on a tourist visa is a grey area; full compliance requires Temporary Residency with work permission for foreign-source income. ^[https://www.mexperience.com/difference-between-temporary-and-permanent-residency-mexico]

### highly_skilled_employment
No separate EU Blue Card-style category. Standard work permit tied to employer.

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Residente Temporal con Permiso de Trabajo
- **Conditions**: Job offer from Mexican employer; employer must register with INM; labor market test may apply.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable; tied to employment.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years legal residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.gob.mx/inm
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (employer sponsorship required; INM processing times vary).

### student_residency
- **Official name**: Residente Temporal por Estudios
- **Conditions**: Enrollment in recognized Mexican institution; proof of financial means.
- **Duration / renewal**: Course duration, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Student years count toward 5-year clock.
- **Official source**: https://www.gob.mx/inm
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: Residencia por Vínculo Familiar
- **Conditions**: Marriage to Mexican citizen or foreign resident; common-law partnership recognized; parent of Mexican child.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–4 years, aligned with sponsor.
- **Path to citizenship**: 2 years (spouse/parent route).
- **Official source**: https://www.gob.mx/inm
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Residente Permanente (direct route for pensioners since July 2025)
- **Conditions**: Since July 2025, only retirees/pensioners may apply for permanent residency directly from abroad. Income ≥ ~$7,300/mo USD or savings ≥ ~$294,000 USD. Non-retirees must complete 4 years of temporary residency first. ^[https://www.expatinsurance.com/articles/mexico-2026-residency-requirements]
- **Duration / renewal**: Permanent — card never expires for adults 18+.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years total legal residence (any temporary years + permanent). Must have 18 months physical presence in last 2 years.
- **Official source**: https://www.gob.mx/inm
- **Community verdict**: Works well (the July 2025 restriction to retirees-only for direct PR was a significant tightening).

### citizenship_by_investment
Not available.

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: 5 years of legal residence (temporary + permanent); 2 years for spouses of Mexicans or parents of Mexican-born children; must be physically present ≥ 18 months in the 2 years preceding application. ^[https://mexicorelocationguide.com/how-to-become-a-mexican-citizen]
- **Language**: Spanish proficiency demonstrated through exam on Mexican history, culture, and civics (Spanish-language).
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted; Mexico does not require renunciation.
- **Naturalization authority**: Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores (SRE).

## Tax
- **Residency trigger**: 183+ days in a calendar year, or center of vital interests in Mexico.
- **Tax residents**: Worldwide income taxed at progressive rates 1.92%–35%.
- **Non-residents**: Only Mexican-source income taxed.
- **Territorial practice**: Mexico is de facto territorial for many expats who stay under 183 days; however, formal residency cards (temporary/permanent) tend to establish tax residency regardless.
- **No special nomad or retiree regime**.

## IT / Digital Nomad
Mexico has no dedicated DN visa. Remote workers on Temporary Residency can work for foreign employers. Key hubs: Mexico City, Playa del Carmen, Puerto Vallarta, Guadalajara, Mérida. Internet infrastructure is good in cities, variable rurally. Cost of living is moderate.

## Yacht / Maritime
Extensive Pacific and Caribbean coastline. Major marinas: La Paz, Puerto Vallarta, Cancún, Cabo San Lucas. Temporary Import Permit (TIP) for foreign-flagged vessels valid up to 10 years. Mexican yacht registration available.

## Notes for RU Citizens
Mexico grants 180-day visa-free entry to Russian passport holders. Temporary Residency applications accepted at Mexican consulates worldwide (some in the region: Kazakhstan, UAE, Turkey). No special restrictions beyond standard solvency requirements. Mexican banks generally open to Russian residents with valid residency card. Russian community present in Mexico City, Cancún, and Playa del Carmen.

## Community Pulse
The dominant complaints are consulate-to-consulate variance in financial thresholds and INM processing delays. The July 2025 tightening of direct permanent residency to retirees-only was significant. The Temporary Residency route remains the standard on-ramp. Mexico's 5-year citizenship timeline is average for the region.

## Status
- **Last monitored**: 2026-09-13
- **Recent changes**: July 2025 — direct permanent residency restricted to retirees/pensioners only. 2026 fee increases — government fees for residency roughly doubled.

## Country Card

### General description
Mexico is a North American country bordered by the United States to the north, the Pacific Ocean to the west and south, and the Gulf of Mexico/Caribbean Sea to the east. It has the second-largest economy in Latin America (after Brazil), driven by manufacturing, oil, tourism, and remittances. Mexico is one of the most popular expat destinations in the world, with large retiree and digital-nomad communities concentrated in Mexico City, the Riviera Maya, Puerto Vallarta, and Lake Chapala. The Temporary Residency via economic solvency is the standard entry route, with a 5-year path to citizenship.

### Well-known strong points
- Low cost of living relative to North America and Europe (Numbeo index 33.5)
- Large, established expat communities with deep infrastructure (healthcare, banking, services)
- Proximity to the United States — short flights, same time zones
- Rich culture, cuisine, and geographic diversity (beaches, mountains, colonial cities)
- 180-day visa-free tourist entry for most Western and many non-Western passport holders
- Territorial tax practice — many residents structure affairs to avoid worldwide taxation

### Well-known problems
- Very high violent crime and homicide rates (25 per 100,000 — among the highest globally)
- Weak rule of law (WJP 0.42) — police corruption, impunity, unreliable judiciary
- Consulate-to-consulate variance in Temporary Residency financial thresholds creates uncertainty
- Cartel-related violence makes some regions effectively off-limits to foreigners
- Bureaucratic inefficiency (INM processing delays, ambiguous requirements)
- Water scarcity and pollution issues in major cities (Mexico City, Monterrey)
