## Overview
Norway is not an EU member but is an EEA/Schengen member with a strong passport. Immigration is managed by the Norwegian Directorate of Immigration (UDI). Norway's residence system is employment-centric: skilled workers can obtain a residence permit with a job offer and relevant qualifications. Self-employed and entrepreneur routes exist. No golden visa or digital nomad visa. Permanent residence after 3 years. Citizenship requires 8 years of residence + language + citizenship test. ^[https://www.udi.no/en]

## Per-Program Detail

### Highly Skilled Employment
- **Skilled worker residence permit** — main route for qualified non-EU/EEA professionals
- **Conditions**: completed university degree (bachelor's minimum) OR completed vocational training of ≥3 years at upper secondary level; concrete job offer from a Norwegian employer; pay and working conditions must match Norwegian standards (minimum baseline: NOK **341,373**/year pre-tax in 2026); the job must normally be full-time and require the qualification. ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/work-immigration/skilled-workers]
- **Duration**: up to **3 years** for degree-requiring positions; 1 year for vocational-level positions or through staffing agencies
- **Employer change**: can change employers within the same type of position without new application (must notify police within 7 days)
- **Job loss**: 6-month grace period to find new employment (permit must remain valid)
- **Path to permanent residence**: after **3 years** of legal residence with a work-based permit (income ≥NOK 317,000/year; Norwegian language A1 minimum; no criminal record) ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/permanent-residence-permit]
- **Path to citizenship**: 8 years
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/work-immigration/skilled-workers]
- **Community verdict**: Works well for qualified professionals; vocational-level applicants from certain countries face extra credential verification

### Startup Entrepreneur
- **Self-employed residence permit** — for entrepreneurs and self-employed persons with a business in Norway
- **Conditions**: must establish and operate a sole proprietorship in Norway; business must be viable and able to support the applicant; relevant qualifications for the type of business; any required public permits or authorisations (e.g., for regulated trades) must be obtained; applicant must actively run the business; no fixed minimum capital but sufficient funds to establish and live. ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/work-immigration/skilled-workers] (self-employed section)
- **Duration**: 1 year initially, renewable
- **Path to permanent residence**: after 3 years
- **Path to citizenship**: 8 years
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/work-immigration/skilled-workers]
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — vague viability criteria; processing can be subjective; small startup ecosystem outside Oslo

### Employment Residency
- **Skilled worker (same scheme)** — covers any qualified employment meeting the education/qualification and salary requirements
- **Conditions**: as above; labour market test not generally required for skilled workers (unlike some EU countries). The scheme applies to employment with a Norwegian employer, intracorporate transfers (max 6 years), and athletes/coaches (separate requirements). ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/work-immigration/skilled-workers]
- **Intracorporate transfer**: for employees of international companies on assignment in Norway. Duration: 2 years at a time, max 6 years. Time on this permit does NOT count toward permanent residence.
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/work-immigration/skilled-workers]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; straightforward for degree-holders; credential verification for vocational applicants is a known pain point

### Student Residency
- **Student residence permit** — for non-EU students
- **Conditions**: admission to a recognised Norwegian educational institution; proof of sufficient funds: NOK **151,690**/year (~€13,000) for living costs (must be deposited in a Norwegian bank account or equivalent); tuition for non-EU students applies (varies by institution, typically NOK 100,000–400,000/year); must have a place to live. ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/studies]
- **Duration**: duration of study programme
- **Work rights**: up to 20 hours/week during term, full-time during holidays
- **Post-graduation**: job-seeking permit up to 1 year (must have graduated from a Norwegian institution)
- **Path to citizenship**: student years count partially (full residence time counts but some permits may not)
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/studies]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; tuition for non-EU is a recent change (2023); high cost of living is the main challenge

### Family Reunification
- **Family reunification** — for nuclear family of legal Norwegian residents
- **Conditions**: sponsor must hold valid residence permit; income requirement: sponsor must have earned ≥NOK **317,000**/year pre-tax (2026) in the prior year and must demonstrate future income at this level; no receipt of social benefits in the last 12 months; adequate housing (own or rent); sponsor must have completed Norwegian language training and social studies (from 2026: for family reunification with a Norwegian citizen or permanent resident). ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/family-immigration]
- **Duration**: tied to sponsor's permit (usually 1–3 years initially, then renewals)
- **Path to permanent residence**: after 3 years
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/family-immigration]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; income threshold is moderate by Norwegian standards; new language requirement for sponsors adds a hurdle

## Citizenship
- **Standard**: 8 years of legal residence in Norway within the last 11 years (with valid permits counting)
- **Reduced**: 6 years if married to a Norwegian citizen or with income above a certain threshold + language/citizenship test passed early
- **Language**: Norwegian (Bokmål or Nynorsk) at A2 written OR B1 spoken (or completed 30 ECTS in Norwegian language at university)
- **Citizenship test**: knowledge of Norwegian society, democratic principles, and history (Statsborgerprøven)
- **Good conduct**: clean criminal record; no immigration violations
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted since 1 January 2020
- **Application**: online via UDI; fee NOK 6,500; processing ~12–24 months
- **EU passport**: no (Norway is EEA but not EU); Norwegian passport is strong — visa-free to ~190 destinations + right to live/work in the EEA ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/norwegian-citizenship]

## Tax
- **Progressive income tax**: bracket tax: 0% up to NOK 221,700, then 1.7%–17.7% on higher brackets. National insurance contribution: 7.8%. Municipal/county tax: ~22% (flat). ALL combined: marginal rate ~22%–47.4%.
- **PAYE (Pay As You Earn) scheme for foreign workers**: simplified flat 25% tax on gross salary for foreign workers in their first year of residence. Conditions: salary ≤NOK 700,000/year. No deductions except standard allowance. Maximum 1 year. ^[https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/taxes/tax-deduction-card-and-advance-tax/i-am-a-foreign-employee/paye]
- **Standard expat regime**: no special long-term expat tax regime beyond PAYE. Foreign employees can claim a standard deduction of 10% of gross salary (max NOK 40,000) for the first 2 years.
- **Corporate tax**: 22%.
- **Wealth tax**: 0.95% municipal + 0.15% state = 1.1% on net wealth above NOK 1,700,000 (2026). Primary residence valued at 25% of market value for wealth tax purposes. Significant for high-net-worth individuals.
- **VAT**: 25% standard.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No dedicated [[digital-nomad]] visa. Remote work is explicitly NOT permitted unless it is part of the employment for which a residence permit was granted. ^[https://www.udi.no/en/want-to-apply/work-immigration/skilled-workers] IT professionals use:
- Skilled worker route (employer-sponsored)
- Self-employed for IT consultants with Norwegian clients
- Oslo is the main tech hub with a strong startup scene. Remote work without Norwegian employment is not a valid residence basis.

## Yacht / Maritime
Norway has one of the world's longest and most dramatic coastlines (fjords, 50,000+ islands). Marine industry is world-class. Yacht registration under Norwegian flag (NIS — Norwegian International Ship Register or NOR — Norwegian Ordinary Ship Register). Major yachting hubs: Oslo (Aker Brygge), Bergen, Stavanger, Ålesund. Recognised captain and maritime licensing.

## Notes for RU citizens
Norway applies EU-aligned sanctions via EEA membership. The land border with Russia (Storskog) is heavily controlled and crossing is restricted. Visa processing is severely restricted for Russian citizens. The skilled worker route is technically open but employer-side commitment and credential verification are significant barriers. Norway has no meaningful Russian-speaking immigration community. The 8-year citizenship path + language requirement is long.
