## Overview
Nigeria operates one of Africa's most structured residency-by-investment frameworks through the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS). The visa classification includes dedicated investor visas (N3A–N3E scaled by investment size), a Highly Skilled Immigrant Visa (N4A), retirement visas (N5A/N5B), and expatriate employment visas (R2A). Citizenship requires 15 years of continuous lawful residence. Dual citizenship is permitted for Nigerian citizens by birth only — NOT for naturalised foreigners. ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/knowledge-base]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency — Investor Visas N3A–N3E
- **Official name**: Permanent Residence Visa — Investor Visa (N3A through N3E and N3E Oil/Gas)
- **Conditions**: Capital imported and retained in Nigeria. N3C (Large Scale Corporations): USD 1M–10M capital, security clearance, business plan, Certificate of Incorporation/CAC, previous F4B/F4C visa. ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/info-center/investor-visa-large-scale-corporations-n3c]
- **Duration**: 90-day entry visa then 6-year residence permit (N3C), renewable. ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/info-center/investor-visa-large-scale-corporations-n3c]
- **Path to citizenship**: 15 years continuous lawful residence. Investor categories are explicitly "eligible to apply for citizenship after meeting certain criteria." ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/knowledge-base]
- **Official source**: https://immigration.gov.ng/knowledge-base
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — the framework is structured but bureaucratic. Real-world experiences vary significantly. Source: NIS knowledge base.

### highly_skilled_employment — N4A Highly Skilled Immigrant Visa
- **Official name**: Highly Skilled Immigrant Visa — N4A
- **Conditions**: Specialised knowledge/technical expertise relevant to Nigeria's economic needs. Letter of recommendation from Nigerian Skills Gap Assessment (under ITF — Industrial Training Fund). Vetted credentials and CV. Police report. ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/info-center/highly-skilled-immigrant-visa-n4a]
- **Duration**: 90-day entry visa, then 5-year residence permit, renewable. ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/info-center/highly-skilled-immigrant-visa-n4a]
- **Path to citizenship**: 15 years continuous lawful residence.
- **Official source**: https://immigration.gov.ng/info-center/highly-skilled-immigrant-visa-n4a
- **Community verdict**: Works well for oil & gas, tech, and finance professionals targeting Lagos or Abuja.

## Citizenship
- Naturalisation: 15 years continuous lawful residence. ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/knowledge-base]
- Good character; security clearance; intent to continue residing in Nigeria.
- Language: English (official).
- Dual citizenship: NOT permitted for naturalised citizens. Nigerian citizens by birth may hold dual citizenship. ^[https://immigration.gov.ng/knowledge-base]
- Passport: visa-free access to ~45 destinations (ECOWAS free movement). Limited global strength.
- **Honest assessment**: The 15-year timeline + prohibition of dual citizenship makes Nigeria one of the least attractive naturalisation destinations. The residency-by-investment framework is robust for those wanting to live and work, but not for passport acquisition.

## Tax
- Residence-based taxation. Tax resident if >183 days or ordinarily resident.
- Personal income tax: progressive 7%–24% (top rate on NGN 3.2M+/yr, ~USD 4,000). Relatively low top rate.
- Corporate tax: 30% (large companies); 20% (medium).
- VAT: 7.5%.
- Capital gains tax: 10%.
- Lagos State has additional consumption taxes.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No dedicated DN visa. Lagos is Africa's largest tech hub (Yaba — "Yabacon Valley") with a vibrant startup ecosystem (Paystack, Flutterwave, Andela). Internet: 4G/LTE stable in Lagos/Abuja, fibre in business areas. Co-working spaces: Workstation, Venia Hub, CcHub. Cost of living: varies dramatically — Lagos can be expensive in expat areas. Remote workers typically enter on business visas or find employer sponsorship. Tourism e-visa available (90 days).

## Yacht / Maritime
Nigeria has an Atlantic coastline (Lagos, Port Harcourt, Calabar). Nigerian Maritime Administration and Safety Agency (NIMASA) handles vessel registration. Security concerns in some offshore areas. Lagos Yacht Club exists. Limited superyacht infrastructure compared to South Africa.

## Notes for RU citizens
Visa required for Russian citizens (e-visa or embassy). Nigeria maintains cordial relations with Russia. Small Russian-speaking community in Lagos and Abuja. Banking: functional but cash-based economy presents challenges. The NIS framework is well-documented. Security: situational awareness essential in certain areas.

## Country Card

Nigeria is Africa's most populous nation (~233M) and its largest economy by GDP, with a structured residency-by-investment framework through the Nigeria Immigration Service (NIS). The visa system includes dedicated investor visas (N3A–N3E scaled by investment size), a Highly Skilled Immigrant Visa (N4A), retirement visas (N5A/N5B), and expatriate employment visas (R2A). Lagos is Africa's largest tech hub ("Yabacon Valley"), home to unicorns like Paystack and Flutterwave. Citizenship requires 15 years. Dual citizenship is not permitted for naturalised foreigners.

### Well-known strong points
- Most structured residency framework in Africa: dedicated investor (N3A–E), skilled (N4A), retirement (N5A/B) visas
- Lagos is Africa's premier tech hub with vibrant startup ecosystem and large market
- English is the official language — no language barrier
- Investor visas range from small enterprise (N3A) to ultra-large (N3D, USD 10M+) with 6-year residence
- Low personal income tax ceiling (24% top rate) compared to regional peers
- Massive domestic market (~233M consumers) — unmatched business opportunity in Africa

### Well-known problems
- 15-year citizenship timeline + prohibition on dual citizenship for naturalised citizens
- No digital nomad visa; remote workers need employer or investor sponsorship
- Security challenges: insurgency (Northeast), kidnapping, and crime in some areas
- Passport is one of Africa's weakest (~45 visa-free); not a passport-shopping destination
- Infrastructure gaps: unreliable power, traffic congestion (Lagos), limited public transport
- Cost of living in Lagos expat areas (VI, Ikoyi) can be very high — USD 400–900 for 1-bed rent
