## Overview
Armenia offers accessible residence permits and a relatively short path to citizenship (currently 3 years of legal residence, though proposed amendments would raise this to 5 years). The country is visa-free for Russian citizens and has become a major relocation hub. Passport strength is moderate (~65 visa-free destinations). Armenia is not in the EU/EEA but has EAEU membership. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

## Residence
Temporary residence cards are issued for up to 1 year, renewable. Permanent residence cards are issued for 5 years. Special residence status (10 years) is available for ethnic Armenians and distinguished individuals. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

In H1 2026, 10,561 residence permits were issued — a 59% increase over H1 2025. Of these, 8,181 were temporary, 2,085 permanent, and 295 special. Russian citizens led all categories. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

Starting November 1, 2026, all residence permit applications will be fully digitalized via a new electronic system, replacing migration.e-gov.am and the EarlyOne app. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

**Visa exemption extension**: As of July 1, 2026, nationals of 111 countries holding a valid residence permit from the US, EU, Schengen, UAE, Bahrain, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, or Oman can enter Armenia visa-free for up to 180 days per year. Valid until July 1, 2027. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

## Citizenship
Under current law, citizenship by naturalization requires 3 years of legal residence. On September 9, 2026, the Ministry of Internal Affairs proposed amendments that would increase this to:
- 5 years for ordinary foreign citizens (up from 3)
- 3 years for close relatives of Armenian citizens and refugees
- 2 years for ethnic Armenians ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json] ^[raw/articles/analyzed-2026-09-13.json]

Armenia permits dual citizenship. The proposal is at public consultation stage on e-draft.am.

## Tax
Armenia has a flat income tax system. IT workers may benefit from special tax incentives under the IT sector support regime. No capital gains tax on securities traded on the Armenian Stock Exchange for qualifying investors.

## IT / Digital Nomad
Armenia does not have a formal [[digital-nomad]] visa, but the growing IT sector and 1% turnover tax for Individual Entrepreneurs (similar to [[georgia]]) make it practical for remote workers, but residence permits based on business registration or employment are practical routes for remote workers. The country's growing tech sector and low cost of living make it attractive. The digitalization of residence applications (effective Nov 2026) will streamline onboarding.

## Yacht / Maritime
Not applicable. Armenia is landlocked.

## Notes for RU citizens
Armenia is one of the most popular destinations for Russian relocants. Russian citizens can enter visa-free and stay 180 days. In H1 2026, 6,085 Russians received temporary residence permits based on employment, and 1,233 received permanent residence. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

No language barrier (Russian is widely spoken). Banking and business registration are accessible. The proposed citizenship law changes (3→5 years) would affect Russian applicants — existing applications may be processed under current rules if filed before amendments take effect. ^[raw/articles/analyzed-2026-09-13.json]
