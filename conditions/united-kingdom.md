## Overview
The United Kingdom is not an EU member (Brexit since 2020) but retains a strong passport and a mature immigration system via UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI). The points-based immigration system covers Skilled Worker, Innovator Founder, Global Talent, and other routes. No golden visa (Tier 1 Investor closed 17 February 2022). The non-dom tax regime was abolished from April 2025, replaced by a 4-year FIG (Foreign Income and Gains) regime. Citizenship: ILR + 1 year (usually 6 years total). ^[https://www.gov.uk/browse/visas-immigration]

## Per-Program Detail

### Investment Residency
- **Tier 1 Investor (CLOSED)** — closed to new applicants from 17 February 2022. Existing holders can extend (2 years) or apply for ILR (2/3/5 years depending on investment level). Historical thresholds: £2,000,000 (5-year ILR), £5,000,000 (3-year), £10,000,000 (2-year). ^[https://www.gov.uk/tier-1-investor]
- **Community verdict**: N/A (closed).

### Highly Skilled Employment
- **Skilled Worker / Global Talent / Scale-up** — multiple routes for high-skill professionals:
  1. **Skilled Worker** (rebadged Tier 2): job at RQF Level 3+ from licensed sponsor; salary ≥£38,700/year or "going rate" for the occupation (whichever higher). PhD/new entrant/tradeable points discounts reduce salary threshold to ~£30,960. English B1. ^[https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa]
  2. **Global Talent**: for exceptional talent/promise in science, engineering, humanities, digital technology, arts. Endorsed by designated body (e.g., Tech Nation/UKRI, British Academy). No salary/sponsorship requirement. ILR after 3 years. ^[https://www.gov.uk/global-talent]
  3. **Scale-up Worker**: for fast-growing UK companies with ≥10 employees and 20%+ annual growth in turnover or headcount over 3 years. Salary ≥£34,600/year. No sponsorship needed after first 6 months. ILR after 5 years. ^[https://www.gov.uk/scale-up-worker-visa]
- **Duration**: up to 5 years (Skilled Worker/Scale-up), up to 5 years (Global Talent)
- **Path to ILR**: 5 years (Skilled Worker/Scale-up), 3 years (Global Talent)
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa]
- **Community verdict**: Works well for qualified professionals; salary threshold increase to £38,700 (April 2024) is high for some sectors

### Startup Entrepreneur
- **Innovator Founder** — for experienced entrepreneurs with an innovative business idea
- **Conditions**: innovative, viable, and scalable business idea; endorsement from an approved endorsing body (business plan review); English B2; maintenance funds £1,270 (held for 28 days). No minimum investment amount, but endorsing bodies assess viability. ^[https://www.gov.uk/innovator-founder-visa]
- **Duration**: 3 years initial, extendable in 3-year blocks; ILR possible after **3 years** (must meet growth criteria: significant achievements, active business, registered with Companies House)
- **Path to citizenship**: ILR + 1 year
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.gov.uk/innovator-founder-visa]
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — endorsement body process adds uncertainty; 3-year ILR path is competitive

### Employment Residency
- **Skilled Worker visa** — standard employment route
- **Conditions**: job offer from Home Office-licensed sponsor; job at RQF Level 3+ (A-level equivalent); salary ≥£38,700/year or going rate for the occupation; English B1; certificate of sponsorship (CoS). Employer pays Immigration Skills Charge (£364–£1,000/year). ^[https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa]
- **Duration**: up to 5 years, extendable; ILR after 5 years
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; salary threshold is high for some regions/industries; sponsorship requirement is a major barrier for smaller employers

### Student Residency
- **Student visa** (formerly Tier 4) — for non-UK students
- **Conditions**: Confirmation of Acceptance for Studies (CAS) from licensed sponsor; English B2 (B1 for below-degree); proof of funds: £1,334/month (up to 9 months) if studying in London, £1,023/month elsewhere. Tuition varies significantly (international fees £10,000–£38,000+/year). ^[https://www.gov.uk/student-visa]
- **Duration**: course length + wrap-up period (1–4 months depending on course length)
- **Work rights**: 20 hours/week during term (degree level) or 10 hours/week (below degree)
- **Graduate route**: 2 years (3 years for PhD) to work without sponsorship — a major attraction
- **Path to citizenship**: student time does NOT count toward ILR residence requirement
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.gov.uk/student-visa]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; high tuition; Graduate route is valued; dependants restricted since January 2024

### Family Reunification
- **Family visa (spouse/partner)** — for partners/spouses of British citizens/settled persons
- **Conditions**: sponsor minimum income ≥**£29,000/year** (announced increase to £38,700/year; currently under review/slow implementation; higher if sponsoring children); English A1 (initial), A2 (extension), B1 (ILR); adequate housing; genuine relationship evidence. ^[https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa]
- **Duration**: 2.5 years → extend 2.5 years → ILR at 5 years (if married to British citizen, ILR is not required before citizenship)
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa]
- **Community verdict**: Problematic — very high income threshold; the £38,700 proposal would exclude a large portion of the population

## Citizenship
- **Standard path**: ILR + 12 months (or immediately if married to British citizen with ILR)
- **Total residence**: typically 5 years (Skilled Worker) + 1 year ILR = 6 years total. 10-year long residence route also available.
- **Language**: English B1 + Life in the UK test
- **Good character**: criminal record, immigration breaches, financial probity scrutinised
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted
- **Application fee**: £1,500 (adult naturalisation) + biometrics £19.20 + citizenship ceremony £80
- **Processing**: ~6 months
- **UK passport**: strong — visa-free to ~190 destinations ^[https://www.gov.uk/british-citizenship]

## Tax
- **Progressive income tax**: 0% up to £12,570 (personal allowance), 20% £12,571–£50,270, 40% £50,271–£125,140, 45% above £125,140. Personal allowance tapers away from £100,000.
- **Non-dom regime abolished** from 6 April 2025. Replaced by FIG (Foreign Income and Gains) regime: new UK residents get 100% relief on foreign income and gains for the first 4 tax years. After 4 years: taxed on worldwide income. This is a residence-based system (no domicile concept). ^[https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/changes-to-the-taxation-of-non-uk-domiciled-individuals]
- **Inheritance tax**: moving from domicile-based to residence-based (transitional).
- **Corporate tax**: 25% (small profits rate 19% for profits ≤£50K).
- **VAT**: 20% standard.
- **Capital gains tax**: 10%–24% (depending on asset and income).
- **No wealth tax**.

## IT / Digital Nomad
**No dedicated digital nomad visa**. UK visitor visas explicitly prohibit remote work for foreign employers while in the UK. Digital nomads typically use:
- Skilled Worker visa (sponsor required)
- Global Talent (Tech Nation — no sponsor, but evidence of exceptional talent/promise required)
- Innovator Founder (own business)
London is one of the world's top tech hubs (Silicon Roundabout, Canary Wharf fintech). Manchester, Edinburgh, and Cambridge also strong. The Graduate route (2–3 years post-study work) is a popular entry point.

## Yacht / Maritime
UK is a global maritime hub with a long coastline. Yacht registration under the Red Ensign (UK Ship Register, part of MCA). Major yachting hubs: Southampton, Cowes (Isle of Wight), Poole, Lymington, London (St Katharine Docks). Recognised captain licensing (RYA/MCA Yachtmaster). The UK flag is a widely used and respected yacht registration.

## Notes for RU citizens
UK sanctions are among the world's strictest against Russian nationals. Visa processing is slow and subject to enhanced scrutiny. The Innovator Founder route is open in principle but endorsement is competitive. The Global Talent route in digital technology (Tech Nation endorsement) is accessible to genuinely exceptional individuals. Family visa minimum income requirement is a significant barrier. The UK has a sizable Russian-speaking community in London and the South East. Banking is extremely difficult for newly arrived Russian nationals.
