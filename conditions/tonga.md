## Overview
Tonga is a constitutional monarchy and the Pacific's only remaining kingdom. It historically ran one of the world's first citizenship-by-investment schemes (the Tongan Protected Person Passport, 1982–1996, ~7,000 passports sold, shut down after a scandal involving Ferdinand Marcos). No CBI program exists today, though a PM-backed 2026 relaunch proposal (USD 190,000 single / USD 220,000 family, designed with Henley & Partners) is politically live but not enacted. ^[https://www.citizenshiptimes.com/programmes/tonga]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Foreign Investment Certification (Foreign Investment Act 2020)
- **Conditions**: Any "overseas person" (individual or company, including a local company with any non-citizen shareholding) must obtain Foreign Investor Certification before doing business in Tonga. Requires passport copy, proposed locations, investment and employment levels, solvency status. ^[https://businessregistries.gov.to/Documentation/TO/How%20to%20register%20as%20a%20foreign%20investor%20in%20Tonga.pdf]
- **Duration / renewal**: Ongoing while business operates.
- **Path to citizenship**: Business presence does not itself grant residence rights; naturalisation (~5 years) applies separately.
- **Official source**: https://businessregistries.gov.to/Documentation/TO/How%20to%20register%20as%20a%20foreign%20investor%20in%20Tonga.pdf
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (certification is straightforward, but no residence-by-investment).

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Employment Visa / Work Permit
- **Conditions**: Employer-sponsored; employer is Tonga-registered and must document unsuccessful local recruitment. Short-term permit (<6 months, non-renewable) for shorter engagements. ^[https://www.citizenshiptimes.com/programmes/tonga]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Counts toward naturalisation residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.citizenshiptimes.com/programmes/tonga
- **Community verdict**: Works well for NGO/hospitality roles; local-recruitment hurdle.

### citizenship_by_investment
Not active. Tonga ran the Tongan Protected Person Passport scheme (1982–1996), selling ~7,000 passports and raising ~USD 26 million before it was shut down. A 2026 proposal (USD 190,000 single / USD 220,000 family of 2–4, Henley & Partners) has been leaked but not passed by Parliament or approved by the King. ^[https://www.citizenshiptimes.com/programmes/tonga]

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: Naturalisation ~5 years of residence (plus good character, stable finances, language/cultural knowledge). ^[https://immigrantinvest.com/passport-index/tonga]
- **Language**: Tongan and/or English.
- **Dual citizenship**: Historically restricted; Tonga has moved toward permitting dual citizenship but verify current status.
- **By marriage**: Marriage to a Tongan citizen is a route to naturalisation.

## Tax
Tonga has no personal income tax for most residents (a consumption tax applies). Foreign-source income is generally not taxed. Verify current rates with the Ministry of Revenue. ^[https://www.goldenvisas.com/tonga]

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa. Short stays are covered by visitor permits; long-term remote work requires an employment or business certification route.

## Yacht / Maritime
Tonga (Vava'u, Nuku'alofa) is a major South Pacific sailing and whale-watching cruising ground; no dedicated yacht-residency program. yacht-captain relevance low–moderate.

## Notes for RU Citizens
Standard consular visa processing; no dedicated restrictions or agreements reported. Tonga remains a watch-and-wait market for investment migration. [[fiji]] and [[samoa]] are more established Pacific options.

## Community Pulse
Essentially no active expat-investor pipeline; the country is tracked mainly for the proposed CBI relaunch. Naturalisation volume is very low and processing is discretionary.

## Country Card

### General description
Tonga is a small Polynesian constitutional monarchy whose king retains real powers (veto, parliament dissolution). It has a modest, remittance- and tourism-dependent economy. It is notable in investment-migration history for its 1980s passport-sale scheme and a live 2026 proposal to relaunch a CBI program.

### Well-known strong points
- Political stability and generally respected civil liberties (FH 79–80/100 Free)
- Low/no personal income tax regime
- Attractive cruising/whale-watching tourism niche
- Reasonable passport (127 visa-free destinations)

### Well-known problems
- No active residency-by-investment or digital-nomad visa
- CBI relaunch is only a proposal (not enacted)
- Small economy heavily dependent on remittances
- Land laws and monarchical powers raise governance concerns

## Status
- **Last monitored**: 2026-10-11
- **Recent changes**: No enacted changes; 2026 CBI relaunch proposal (Henley & Partners) under discussion.
- **Watch items**: Whether Parliament/King approve the USD 190,000 CBI proposal.
