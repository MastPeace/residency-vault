## Overview
The British Virgin Islands (BVI) is a British Overseas Territory and a leading offshore corporate-registration and yachting jurisdiction. It has **no income tax** (payroll tax and stamp duty apply instead). There is no citizenship-by-investment programme, and full "belonger status" (the local equivalent of permanent residence/right of abode) is tightly restricted, typically requiring ~15–20 years of residence. Foreigners generally live there on work permits or as independent-means residents. ^[https://www.bvi.gov.vg/]

## Per-Program Detail

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Certificate of Residence (non-belonger / independent means)
- **Conditions**: Demonstrate sufficient independent financial means to reside without working; the certificate confers residence without the right to work. Details published by the BVI Immigration Department. ^[https://www.bvi.gov.vg/]
- **Duration / renewal**: Renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Time may count toward the long belonger-status clock (20+ years).
- **Official source**: https://www.bvi.gov.vg/
- **Community verdict**: No reliable data (small applicant pool).

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Work permit
- **Conditions**: Employer-sponsored; most non-belongers must hold a work permit to live and work. No fast track to belonger status. ^[https://www.bvi.gov.vg/]
- **Duration / renewal**: Tied to employment; renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Belonger status after ~15–20 years' residence (discretionary).
- **Official source**: https://www.bvi.gov.vg/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (work permits accessible; belonger status very slow).

## Citizenship
Citizens are British Overseas Territories Citizens (BOTC) and, since 2002, generally also full British citizens (British passport). Local "belonger status" — required to own land without a licence and to vote — is granted only after long residence (commonly ~15–20 years) and is discretionary. Naturalisation as BOTC is possible after 5 years' residence. ^[https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/british-overseas-territories-citizens/british-overseas-territories-citizens-accessible-version]

## Tax
**0% income tax, 0% capital gains, 0% inheritance.** Instead: payroll tax (employer ~4–14%, employee up to 14%) and stamp duty on property (4–12%). No income-tax filing. ^[https://www.bvi.gov.vg/]

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa. Remote workers generally need a work permit or the independent-means certificate (no work right), so the BVI is not a practical nomad base.

## Yacht / Maritime
The BVI is one of the world's premier sailing/yachting destinations (Tortola, Virgin Gorda, Anegada) and a leading yacht-flag registry (Red Ensign group). Charter and crew work permits are the practical entry route for yachting professionals.

## Notes for RU Citizens
No CBI or fast track. Work permits (including yachting/charter) and the independent-means certificate are the realistic routes. The BVI is not a convenient base due to the restricted belonger-status regime.

## Community Pulse
Discussion is dominated by yachting professionals (crew permits, the BVI's sailing season) and offshore-company formation. Residence for lifestyle purposes is limited by the slow belonger-status path and the absence of a nomad visa.

## Country Card

### General description
The British Virgin Islands is a small, affluent Overseas Territory in the eastern Caribbean, best known as the world's leading offshore company-registration hub and a premier sailing destination. Its economy rests on financial services and tourism, with a large transient expatriate workforce in both sectors.

### Well-known strong points
- Zero income/capital-gains/inheritance tax
- British legal system and BOTC/British citizenship route
- World's top offshore company registry and yachting ground
- Safe, stable, English-speaking

### Well-known problems
- Belonger status extremely slow (~15–20 years) and discretionary
- No digital-nomad or investor-residence route
- Payroll tax and high property stamp duty offset the zero income tax
- Small population, limited non-tourism economy; hurricane exposure
