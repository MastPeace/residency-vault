## Overview
Angola is a Lusophone (Portuguese-speaking) oil state on the Atlantic coast of Southern Africa. Immigration is governed by Law No. 13/2019 (Legal Regime of Foreign Citizens). There is no digital-nomad or retirement visa; residence runs through work permits, a "Privileged Visa" for approved investors, and family ties. Citizenship requires 10 years of residence. Dual nationality has been permitted since 1991. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_nationality_law]

Related: [[mozambique]] (fellow Lusophone state), [[south-africa]], [[employment-residency]].

## Per-Program Detail

### employment_residency — Work Visa + Residence Permit
- **Official name**: Visto de Trabalho (Work Visa) + Autorização de Residência
- **Conditions**: Binding job offer; employer must sponsor and justify hiring a foreigner. Governed by Law No. 13/2019. ^[https://www.refworld.org/legal/legislation/natlegbod/2019/122788]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–2 years, renewable, tied to the contract.
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years residence. ^[https://www.refworld.org/legal/legislation/natlegbod/2019/122788]
- **Official source**: https://www.sme.gov.ao (Serviço de Migração e Estrangeiros)
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — slow, bureaucratic; Portuguese language essential. Small expat base outside oil sector.

### investment_residency — Privileged Visa
- **Official name**: Visto Privilegiado (Privileged Visa)
- **Conditions**: Linked to an approved investment project (ANIP/AIPEX — private investment agency). No fixed public minimum widely published; discretionary. ^[https://www.ibn.co.za/angola]
- **Duration / renewal**: Renewable; can lead to permanent residence.
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.aipex.gov.ao
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — oil-and-gas led; practical access limited.

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: 10 years lawful residence for naturalisation. ^[https://publications.iom.int/system/files/pdf/MGI-Angola-2021.pdf]
- **Language**: Portuguese (official). No formal published exam; integration assessed.
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted since 1991 (foreign nationality simply has no legal effect within Angola). ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_nationality_law]
- **Passport**: ~53 destinations visa-free or on arrival.

## Tax
- Residence-based; tax resident if >183 days or habitual abode in Angola.
- Personal income tax (IRT): progressive 0%–17% (top rate on the highest bracket).
- Corporate tax: 25% standard. VAT: 14%.
- Oil/mining sectors have separate regimes. No special expat flat-tax scheme.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa. Internet and cost of living in Luanda are high for the region. Remote workers generally need an employer route or investor permit. Portuguese is essential.

## Yacht / Maritime
Long Atlantic coastline (~1,600 km) with Luanda and Lobito ports. Limited leisure-yacht infrastructure; not a major yachting destination.

## Notes for RU citizens
Visa required. Angola maintains historically strong relations with Russia (Soviet-era ties); some direct business links remain. Banking is difficult and USD/Luanda cost of living high. Portuguese is the working language — English is rarely sufficient.

## Community pulse
Very little English-language expat forum data. The expat presence is concentrated in oil, mining and infrastructure. No meaningful digital-nomad community; immigration is widely reported as slow and document-heavy.

## Country Card

### General description
Angola is a large, oil-rich Lusophone country on the Atlantic coast of Southern Africa. Its economy is dominated by petroleum and diamonds, with Luanda among the most expensive cities on the continent. Immigration is work- and investment-led, with no digital-nomad or retirement route. Citizenship requires 10 years of residence, but dual nationality is permitted.

### Well-known strong points
- Dual citizenship permitted (since 1991) — rare in Africa
- 10-year path to citizenship is relatively short by African standards
- Oil/mining economy supports expat employment niches
- Long Atlantic coastline and Portuguese-language continuity with Portugal and Brazil
- Privileged Visa available for approved investors

### Well-known problems
- No digital-nomad or passive-income visa
- Luanda cost of living is very high; banking and transfers are difficult
- Bureaucratic, slow, document-heavy immigration process
- Freedom House 28/100 (Not Free); weak rule of law (WJP rank 115/142)
- Portuguese language is effectively mandatory
- Median incomes very low outside the oil sector
