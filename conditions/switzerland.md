## Overview
Switzerland is not an EU member but a Schengen member with one of the world's strongest passports and an exceptionally high-wage economy. Immigration is managed by the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) and cantonal migration offices. Switzerland operates a quota system for non-EU/EFTA nationals (8,500 permits/year split between B and L permits, as of 2026). B-permit is the standard residence; C-permit is permanent settlement. Citizenship requires 10 years of residence + cantonal/communal approval. Switzerland is globally attractive for its lump-sum taxation regime. ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]

## Per-Program Detail

### Investment Residency / Retired Residency
- **Lump-sum taxation residence** *(forfait fiscal / Pauschalsteuer, Article 14 of the Federal Act on Foreign Nationals)* — for non-working wealthy individuals who agree to pay tax based on their living expenses rather than worldwide income/wealth
- **Conditions**: no gainful employment in Switzerland (passive investment management permitted); must not have been a Swiss resident in the last 10 years; negotiate a fiscal agreement with the cantonal tax authority. The taxable base is calculated on the taxpayer's annual living expenses, with a minimum typically CHF 400,000+ in federal + cantonal + communal taxes combined (varies significantly by canton: CHF 200,000+ in cheaper cantons, CHF 1,000,000+ in Zurich/Geneva). ^[https://www.rsm.global/switzerland/en/service/tax-and-legal/private-clients-services/private-clients-services/lump-sum-taxation]
- **Quota**: subject to cantonal + federal non-EU quotas (roughly 4,500 B permits/year nationally for non-EU)
- **Duration**: B permit (renewable annually, then C permit after 10 years)
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years + cantonal/communal approval (lump-sum status may pose integration questions during naturalisation)
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Community verdict**: Works well (established, reliable, predictable); very high wealth threshold; canton choice is crucial

### Highly Skilled Employment
- **B permit (résidence avec activité lucrative)** — for qualified non-EU/EFTA professionals
- **Conditions**: job offer from Swiss employer; cantonal quota available (non-EU: 4,500 B permits + 4,000 L permits/year nationally, allocated across cantons); employer must demonstrate no suitable Swiss/EU/EFTA candidate (labour market test); salary and working conditions must match Swiss market standards; professional qualifications (university degree or specialised expertise). ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Duration**: B permit 1–5 years; C permit (permanent) after 10 years continuous residence (5 years for US/Canadian citizens, or through accelerated integration)
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years residence
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Community verdict**: Works well for highly qualified professionals; quota system creates real constraints; Swiss employers must be genuinely motivated to hire non-EU candidates

### Startup Entrepreneur
- **B permit (self-employed / indépendant)** — for entrepreneurs establishing a business in Switzerland
- **Conditions**: detailed business plan demonstrating economic benefit to Switzerland (job creation, innovation, tax revenue); cantonal economic development office assessment; quota availability; sufficient start-up capital; professional qualifications relevant to the business. Canton-level decisions — some cantons (Zug, Zurich, Vaud) are more entrepreneur-friendly. ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Duration**: B permit 1–2 years initially, renewable
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — canton-dependent; strong business plan and capital are essential; Zurich/Zug are smoother than smaller cantons

### Employment Residency
- **L permit (short-term)** and **B permit (résidence)** — for employed non-EU nationals
- **L permit**: for employment contracts up to 1 year; tied to employer; subject to annual quota (4,000 nationally). Renewable up to 24 months total.
- **B permit**: for employment >1 year; tied to employer initially; subject to quota (4,500/year). After 2 years, no longer tied to the employer. ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Conditions**: job offer; quota availability (employer applies to canton); salary ≥ market rate; employer proves no suitable Swiss/EU candidate
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; quota bottleneck is the main risk; large multinational employers manage the process efficiently

### Student Residency
- **B permit (études)** — for non-EU students
- **Conditions**: admission to a recognised Swiss university (ETH Zurich, EPFL, University of Zurich, University of Geneva, etc.); proof of sufficient funds (~CHF 21,000/year); evidence of intent to return after studies; health insurance. ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Duration**: duration of study programme
- **Work rights**: max 15 hours/week during term, full-time during holidays; only after 6 months of residence
- **Post-graduation**: 6-month job-seeking permit; if hired in a qualified role, transition to B permit (subject to quotas)
- **Path to citizenship**: student years count toward 10-year residence
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; world-class universities; high cost of living is the main challenge

### Family Reunification
- **Family reunification** — for nuclear family of legal Swiss residents
- **Conditions**: sponsor must hold valid B/C permit; adequate housing (communal minimum square metre requirements); sufficient income to support family; family members apply at Swiss embassy abroad. Family members receive dependent B permit, often with work authorisation. ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Duration**: tied to sponsor's permit
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years (or 5 years after marriage to Swiss citizen — simplified naturalisation)
- **Official source**: ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/themen/aufenthalt/nicht_eu_efta.html]
- **Community verdict**: Works well; dependent B permit includes work rights in most cantons

## Citizenship
- **Standard (ordinary naturalisation)**: 10 years of continuous residence in Switzerland (including 3 of the last 5 years before application). Years between ages 8–18 count double (but minimum 6 years actual residence required).
- **Three-level approval**: federal + canton + commune. The commune is the most unpredictable — requires integration, language skills, and often a vote or interview on local knowledge/participation.
- **Language**: at least B1 spoken + A2 written in the official language of the commune of residence (German/French/Italian). Some communes require B2.
- **Civic knowledge**: cantonal and communal knowledge assessed at interview
- **Good conduct**: clean criminal record; no debts or bankruptcy; compliance with Swiss laws and values
- **Cost**: federal ~CHF 100 + cantonal/communal CHF 500–3,000 (varies widely)
- **Dual citizenship**: permitted
- **Simplified naturalisation**: spouse of Swiss citizen — 5 years residence + 3 years of marriage. Under-25 individuals with 3 generations of Swiss residence.
- **EU passport**: no (Switzerland is not EU) but Swiss passport is exceptionally strong (visa-free to ~190 destinations, Schengen) ^[https://www.sem.admin.ch/sem/en/home/integration-einbuergerung/schweizer-werden/ordentlich.html]

## Tax
Switzerland is a federal system with three levels of taxation: federal, cantonal, and communal. This creates significant tax competition between cantons.

- **Federal income tax**: progressive, 0%–11.5% (top rate above ~CHF 900,000). Effective rate typically 1%–5% for most earners.
- **Cantonal/communal income tax**: highly variable — total effective rate (federal + canton + commune) ranges from ~12% (Zug, Schwyz) to ~45% (Geneva) for high incomes.
- **Lump-sum taxation (forfait fiscal)**: non-working wealthy foreign residents pay tax on their living expenses instead of worldwide income/wealth. Minimum taxable base varies by canton. Must not work in Switzerland. Re-negotiated periodically with canton. ~4,500 people use this regime as of 2025. ^[https://steuern.lu.ch/natuerlichepersonen/Wissenswertes_NP/besteuerung_aufwand/lump_sum_taxation]
- **Corporate tax**: federal 8.5% effective + cantonal ~1.5%–14% (total 10%–22%, depending on canton). Cantons like Zug, Lucerne, Nidwalden are very low.
- **Wealth tax**: cantonal, typically 0.1%–1% on net assets above a tax-free allowance (varies by canton).
- **VAT**: 8.1% standard (lowest in Europe); 2.6% reduced.
- **No capital gains tax** on private securities (real estate gains taxed separately).
- **Tax ruling practice**: cantonal tax rulings provide advance certainty on tax treatment — a major attraction for businesses and wealthy individuals.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No dedicated [[digital-nomad-visa|digital nomad visa]] visa. Switzerland has a thriving tech/IT scene (Google Zurich, ETH/EPFL spin-offs, Crypto Valley in Zug). Remote work for a foreign employer alone does NOT qualify for a Swiss residence permit — you need a Swiss employer or a self-employed business with Swiss economic benefit. IT professionals use:
- B permit through Swiss employer sponsorship
- Self-employed B permit (freelance/consulting for Swiss clients)
- Zurich, Zug, Lausanne (EPFL), Geneva are major tech hubs

## Yacht / Maritime
Switzerland is landlocked. No maritime registry. Swiss yacht owners typically register under other flags (UK Red Ensign, Cayman Islands, Malta, etc.). Swiss residents are major yacht owners globally, but no domestic yachting infrastructure.

## Notes for RU citizens
Switzerland applies EU-equivalent sanctions against Russia. Non-EU quota applies to Russian nationals (no special carve-out). The lump-sum taxation route is technically accessible but Swiss banks are extremely cautious with Russian-source wealth (freezing orders, compliance burdens). For skilled workers: Swiss employer must justify hiring a non-EU candidate over 500M+ EU/EEA workers. Communal citizenship approval is the most unpredictable — some communes have a reputation for rejecting applicants perceived as insufficiently integrated. The 10-year path is long. Switzerland has a small but established Russian-speaking community (Geneva, Zurich, Zug).

## Country Card

Switzerland is a landlocked, non-EU Schengen country with one of the world's highest standards of living, a uniquely decentralised political system, and an exceptionally competitive tax environment due to inter-cantonal tax competition. Its immigration system is quota-constrained for non-EU nationals (8,500 permits/year) and heavily employment-driven, with the notable exception of the lump-sum taxation regime (forfait fiscal) for wealthy non-working residents. Citizenship is among Europe's most demanding: 10 years of residence plus approval at federal, cantonal, and communal levels. The Swiss passport is one of the strongest globally (186 visa-free destinations).

### Well-known strong points
- Very low personal income tax in competitive cantons (Zug, Schwyz: ~12% total effective rate)
- Lump-sum taxation regime for wealthy non-working foreign residents — globally unique
- World-class financial centre, political stability, and strong currency (CHF)
- Exceptionally high wages: median net salary ~CHF 6,000/month
- Top-tier infrastructure, healthcare, education (ETH Zurich/EPFL), and public transport
- No capital gains tax on private securities

### Well-known problems
- Extremely high cost of living — Numbeo index above NYC (109)
- 10-year citizenship path with three-level approval (communal vote can reject)
- Strict non-EU quotas (8,500/year) create real barriers to entry
- No digital nomad or passive-income visa; remote work for foreign employers not a residence basis
- Housing shortage and very high rents in Zurich, Geneva, and major cities
- Landlocked — no maritime access; cantonal wealth tax applies (0.1%–1% on net assets)
