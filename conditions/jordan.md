## Overview
Jordan is one of the few countries in the Middle East that offers BOTH residency by investment and citizenship by investment. The programme was reformed in July 2025 and further amended in July 2026, with clear investment thresholds. Jordan permits dual citizenship. Income tax is progressive (5%–30%). A stable constitutional monarchy with strong Western ties, Jordan provides access to a USD-pegged currency (JOD) and strategic Middle East positioning. ^[https://invest.jo/sites/default/files/2026-08/Jordanian-Citizenship-Through-Investment-English.pdf]

## Per-Program Detail

### 5-Year Property Residency
- **Official name**: 5-Year Renewable Residency Through Property Ownership
- **Conditions** (reformed July 2026): ^[https://invest.jo/sites/default/files/2026-08/Jordanian-Citizenship-Through-Investment-English.pdf]
  - Property from licensed developer: ≥ JD 200,000 (~$282,000)
  - Property from non-developer: ≥ JD 300,000 (~$423,000)
  - Property outside Amman Governorate: ≥ JD 150,000 (~$212,000)
  - Property held 5 years without sale or mortgage.
  - Renewal: continue to own same property or acquire equivalent-value property.
- **Duration / renewal**: 5 years, renewable. No physical presence requirement. ^[https://imidaily.com/jordan-investor-residency]
- **Path to citizenship**: No direct path. Must use separate citizenship-by-investment route.
- **Official source**: https://invest.jo
- **Community verdict**: Works well — fast (14-day processing), no physical presence, wide family inclusion (parents, adult unmarried daughters, sons under 30).

### Citizenship by Investment
- **Official name**: منح الجنسية الأردنية من خلال الاستثمار (Jordanian Citizenship Through Investment)
- **Conditions** (amended July 2026): ^[https://jordantimes.com/news/local/cabinet-approves-new-investor-residency-citizenship-rules]
  - **Share purchase**: JD 1,500,000 (~$2,100,000) in new shares of Jordanian companies. Max 10% in one company. Hold 5 years. Within 4 months of approval.
  - **New project — Amman**: JD 700,000 (~$990,000) capital in new project. Grants 3-year temporary passport, then full citizenship.
  - **New project — outside Amman**: JD 500,000 (~$705,000) capital. Same structure.
  - **Existing project injection**: JD 1,000,000 (~$1,400,000) in shares, with JD 500,000 in new fixed assets. Hold 3 years. 20+ jobs created.
  - **Pharmaceuticals/food logistics**: JD 3,000,000 (~$4,200,000).
  - **Employment route**: 150 jobs in Amman or 100 outside Amman.
  - **Amra City Project**: JD 1,500,000.
  - Citizenship extends to: spouse, dependent daughters, parents, unmarried sons under 24 (under 30 if investment > JD 2M).
- **Duration / renewal**: Citizenship is permanent. 681 investors naturalized since programme began in 2018. ^[https://jordantimes.com/news/local/cabinet-approves-new-investor-residency-citizenship-rules]
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted. ^[https://imidaily.com/jordan-investor-residency]
- **Official source**: https://invest.jo (Ministry of Investment)
- **Community verdict**: Established programme with track record of approvals. The most accessible citizenship-by-investment programme in the Middle East.

### Employment Residency
- **Official name**: Work Permit / Residency
- **Conditions**: Employer sponsor, valid labour contract.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Not through standard employment; years of residence may count toward naturalization (15 years for Arabs, 25 years for others).
- **Official source**: https://moi.gov.jo
- **Community verdict**: Standard.

### Student Residency
- **Official name**: Student Visa
- **Conditions**: Enrolment at Jordanian institution.
- **Duration / renewal**: Duration of programme.
- **Official source**: https://moi.gov.jo
- **Community verdict**: Functional.

### Family Reunification
- **Official name**: Family Joining
- **Conditions**: Sponsor holds valid residency/citizenship.
- **Duration / renewal**: Tied to sponsor.
- **Official source**: https://moi.gov.jo
- **Community verdict**: Standard.

## Citizenship
- **Citizenship by investment**: See above — multiple routes, JD 500K–1.5M.
- **Naturalization** (standard):
  - Arabs: 15 years of legal residence + Arabic proficiency.
  - Non-Arabs: 25 years of legal residence + Arabic proficiency.
  - Women married to Jordanian men: 3 years (non-Arab) or 5 years (Arab woman).
  - All: Cabinet approval required, discretionary.
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted.
- **Jordanian passport**: ~55 visa-free destinations. US E-2 treaty investor eligible. Access to GCC labour market. ^[https://uglobal.com/immigration/egypt]

## Tax
- **Progressive income tax**: 5%–30%.
  - 5% on first JD 5,000
  - 10% up to JD 10,000
  - 15% up to JD 15,000
  - 20% up to JD 20,000
  - 25% up to JD 1,000,000
  - 30% above JD 1,000,000
- **Corporate tax**: 20% (general); 35% (banks); 15% (industrial).
- **VAT**: 16% (general rate).
- **No wealth tax, no inheritance tax.**
- **Property tax**: 10% on rental income; capital gains on property sale.
- Tax residency: 183+ days or centre of vital interests.
- Double taxation treaties: ~35 treaties.

## IT / Digital Nomad
- **No dedicated digital nomad visa.** Jordan does not offer a remote work visa.
- IT sector is growing: King Hussein Business Park, Aqaba Special Economic Zone.
- Internet infrastructure: good in Amman (fibre, 4G/5G), weaker in rural areas.
- For remote workers: tourist visa (visa-free/eVisa for many nationalities, 30–90 days).
- 5-year property residency can be used as a base for remote workers (no physical presence required).
- Cost of living is low compared to Gulf: comfortable lifestyle at $1,500–2,500/mo.

## Yacht / Maritime
- Aqaba on the Red Sea: Jordan's only seaport. Modest marina facilities.
- Tala Bay Marina (Aqaba) — small-scale yachting.
- Jordan-flag registration available.
- Red Sea access to Egypt, Israel, Saudi Arabia.

## Notes for RU citizens
- **Visa on arrival** or **eVisa** for Russian passport holders (30 days, extendable). Easy entry.
- **Growing RU presence** — Amman and Aqaba seeing Russian investment interest.
- **Jordan accessible to Russian banking** — no sanctions; Jordanian dinar pegged to USD provides stability.
- **Citizenship by investment programme open to RU nationals** — JD 500K outside Amman is the most affordable entry point in the Middle East for citizenship.
- **Jordanian citizenship provides E-2 eligibility** — indirect path to US residence for RU nationals (though RU origin complicates US visa processing).
- **Amman quality of life**: moderate cost, good healthcare, English widely spoken, culturally moderate.
- **Limited direct flights**: Amman–Moscow via Royal Jordanian (~3 weekly). Connections via Istanbul/Dubai common.

## Community pulse
Jordan's investment residency and citizenship programmes are functional and well-established. The 5-year property residency with no physical presence requirement is unique in the region and practical for investors seeking parallel residence bases. The citizenship-by-investment programme is the most accessible in the Middle East (JD 500K outside Amman). Main drawbacks: weaker passport (~55 visa-free), limited job market, and political tensions in the broader region. For those prioritizing MENA second citizenship, Jordan is a credible option.

## Status
- **Last monitored**: 2026-10-26
- **Recent changes**: Cabinet amended investor citizenship/residency bylaws (Jul 2026). New routes: JD 500K project outside Amman; JD 700K in Amman. Property residency thresholds adjusted. 681 total naturalizations since 2018 programme launch.
- **Watch items**: Continued reform of investment thresholds. Potential introduction of digital nomad or retirement visa.

## Country Card

Jordan is a stable constitutional monarchy in the Levant, bordered by Israel, Syria, Iraq, and Saudi Arabia. It lacks oil wealth but has positioned itself as a regional hub for IT, medical tourism, and expatriate living. The cost of living is among the lowest in the Middle East, and Amman has a growing startup scene. Jordan offers citizenship pathways — 15 years for non-Arabs, 4 years for Arab nationals, and investment-based routes (JD 500K–700K). The country hosts a large refugee population (Palestinian, Syrian, Iraqi) that shapes its demographic and economic landscape.

### Well-known strong points
- Low cost of living — one of the most affordable countries in the Middle East
- Citizenship pathway exists: naturalization possible (15 years) and investment routes available
- Political stability in a volatile region — constitutional monarchy with relative predictability
- Strategic location: access to Gulf, Levant, and North African markets
- English widely spoken among educated population; strong IT/BPO sector
- Rich history and tourism: Petra, Wadi Rum, Dead Sea, Jerash

### Well-known problems
- Weak passport (49 visa-free) — Jordanian citizenship adds limited mobility
- Low salaries (median ~$500–700/mo) — limited domestic earning potential for professionals
- High unemployment (~22%), especially among youth and graduates
- Water scarcity is severe — one of the world's most water-poor countries
- Heavy refugee burden strains infrastructure, education, and labour market
- Bureaucracy and wasta (connections) culture impede business efficiency
