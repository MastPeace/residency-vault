## Overview
Gabon is a sparsely populated, oil-rich Central African country on the Atlantic coast (Francophone, CEMAC/CFA franc zone). It offers no citizenship- or residence-by-investment programme; residence is via work permits, and naturalisation is possible after a period of residence (spouses after 3 years of marriage). Dual nationality has been permitted since 1962. High GDP per capita masks deep inequality and a "Not Free" political system. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabonese_nationality_law]

## Per-Program Detail

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Permis de séjour / work permit (Code du travail)
- **Conditions**: Binding job offer from a Gabonese employer; employer sponsorship. Published thresholds are thin; oil-sector expatriates dominate.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Naturalisation after a residence period (5 years commonly cited; spouse of a Gabonese national after 3 years of marriage). ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabonese_nationality_law]
- **Official source**: https://www.droit-afrique.com/upload/doc/gabon/Gabon-Code-de-la-nationalite-1998.pdf
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — oil-sector postings work well; bureaucracy slow; French fluency required.

## Citizenship
- **Naturalisation**: The Nationality Code (Loi No. 37-1998) permits naturalisation for persons with sufficient residence to "understand the customs and traditions"; a specific general residence period is not clearly published (5 years commonly cited). Spouses naturalise after 3 years of marriage. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabonese_nationality_law]
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted since 1962. The president must be Gabonese of origin (four generations). ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabonese_nationality_law]
- **Passport**: ~56 visa-free/on-arrival destinations. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabonese_nationality_law]

## Tax
- Corporate income tax: 30% standard. VAT: 18%. Personal income tax progressive (IRPP). Residence-based taxation within the CEMAC/CFA franc zone. Tax data for remote workers are thin — confidence low.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No dedicated digital-nomad visa. Libreville is a regional oil/business hub, not a tech or nomad destination. Remote workers enter on business visas or via employer sponsorship. French fluency essential.

## Yacht / Maritime
Atlantic coastline with ports at Libreville and Port-Gentil (oil terminal). Limited leisure-yachting infrastructure. Not a yachting destination.

## Notes for RU citizens
- Visa required; French working language. Neutral-to-friendly diplomatic posture historically.
- No residence-by-investment; work permits are employer-driven (largely oil/mining).
- CFA franc (pegged to EUR) provides monetary stability.

## Community Pulse
Very little expat-residency activity outside the oil and mining sectors. No digital-nomad or investor-residence community to speak of. The 2023 coup and subsequent transition added political risk to an already thin migration story.

## Country Card

### General description
Gabon is a small-population, oil-rich Central African state on the Atlantic coast, in the Francophone CFA-franc zone. Its high GDP per capita is concentrated in oil and timber and coexists with deep inequality. It is relevant to migrants mainly through expatriate oil/mining postings; there is no investment-residence or citizenship-by-investment route.

### Well-known strong points
- High GDP per capita (~$19,000 PPP) for the region
- CFA franc monetary stability (EUR peg)
- Dual citizenship permitted since 1962
- Abundant natural resources and rainforest cover

### Well-known problems
- No residence-by-investment or CBI programme
- "Not Free" political system (FH 21/100); 2023 coup and transition
- Weak rule of law (WJP rank 122/143)
- Deep inequality despite oil wealth
- Thin published immigration rules; French fluency required
