## Overview
Egypt is one of the few MENA countries with BOTH a residency-by-investment framework and a citizenship-by-investment programme (Law No. 190 of 2019, amended 2023). The CBI programme starts from $250,000 with four routes: donation, real estate ($300,000), business project ($350,000+$100,000 deposit), or bank deposit ($500,000). Residency-by-investment through property can be as low as ~$50,000. Egypt permits dual citizenship in practice (does not enforce renunciation). ^[https://csglobalpartners.com/citizenship-investment/egypt]

## Per-Program Detail

### Residence by Investment
- **Official name**: Residence by Investment (real estate / business / bank deposit)
- **Conditions**: Multiple routes, no single unified programme. ^[https://adamfayed.com/blog/immigration-citizenship/egypt-residence-by-investment]
  - **Real estate**: Property purchases from ~$50,000 commonly linked to 1-year residence; $100,000–200,000 for 3–5 year permits. Properties in government-licensed projects. Under March 2023 decree, residency through real estate formally recognized.
  - **Bank deposit**: Fixed deposit in Egyptian bank, thresholds vary (typically $50,000+).
  - **Business/company**: Investment project registered under Investment Law No. 72/2017. Non-Egyptian investors granted residence for the term of the project.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–5 years, renewable. Tied to investment maintenance.
- **Path to citizenship**: Residence does not lead to citizenship. Separate CBI or naturalization routes.
- **Official source**: https://gafi.gov.eg (General Authority for Investment and Free Zones)
- **Community verdict**: Fragmented but functional. Real estate route is the most commonly used. No single authoritative programme.

### Citizenship by Investment
- **Official name**: Egyptian Citizenship by Investment (Law No. 190 of 2019, amended 2023) — administered by Citizenship by Investment Unit at the Cabinet.
- **Conditions** — four routes: ^[https://csglobalpartners.com/citizenship-investment/egypt]
  - **Donation**: $250,000 direct revenue to state treasury (non-refundable).
  - **Real estate**: $300,000+ in government-licensed project. Hold 5 years. If sold before 5 years: deposit $250,000 to retain citizenship.
  - **Investment project**: $350,000 in investment project + $100,000 deposit as direct revenue. Under Investment Law. Total: $450,000.
  - **Bank deposit**: $500,000 in 3-year interest-bearing deposit at Central Bank (refundable in EGP after 3 years).
  - All investments must be in USD, transferred from abroad.
  - Requirements: Passport, clean criminal record, medical report, proof of income source. No language test, no residency requirement, no interview. ^[https://uglobal.com/immigration/egypt]
- **Duration / renewal**: Citizenship granted directly (6–12 months processing). No prior residency required. President signs citizenship decrees.
- **Dual citizenship**: Egypt formally requires renunciation but does NOT enforce it in practice — dual citizenship is de facto tolerated. ^[https://csglobalpartners.com/citizenship-investment/egypt]
- **Official source**: Citizenship by Investment Unit, Cabinet of Egypt
- **Community verdict**: Established programme. Relatively affordable (from $250,000). Processing 6–12 months is slower than Caribbean CBI but functional.

### Employment Residency
- **Official name**: Work Permit + Residence
- **Conditions**: Employer sponsor, labour contract. Ministry of Manpower approval. Labour market considerations apply.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years continuous residence for naturalization.
- **Official source**: https://www.manpower.gov.eg
- **Community verdict**: Standard bureaucratic process.

### Student Residency
- **Official name**: Student Visa
- **Conditions**: Enrolment at Egyptian institution.
- **Duration / renewal**: Duration of programme.
- **Official source**: https://www.mohe.gov.eg
- **Community verdict**: Functional.

### Family Reunification
- **Official name**: Family Residence
- **Conditions**: Sponsor holds valid residence or Egyptian citizenship.
- **Duration / renewal**: Tied to sponsor.
- **Official source**: Ministry of Interior — Passports, Immigration and Nationality Administration.
- **Community verdict**: Standard.

## Citizenship
- **Citizenship by Investment**: See above — $250,000 donation route is the floor.
- **Naturalization** (standard): 10 years continuous legal residence + integration (Arabic proficiency, good conduct, self-sufficiency). Discretionary — Minister of Interior decision. ^[https://shuralawfirm.com/en/conditions-for-obtaining-egyptian-citizenship-through-investment/]
- **Birth**: Born to Egyptian father (paternal descent). Limited maternal transmission.
- **Marriage**: Foreign wife of Egyptian citizen may obtain citizenship after 2 years of marriage + residence (Ministerial decree). Foreign husband: no equivalent right.
- **Egyptian passport**: ~49 visa-free destinations. Relatively weak. US E-2 Treaty Investor Visa eligible (bilateral investment treaty). ^[https://uglobal.com/immigration/egypt]
- **Dual citizenship**: De facto permitted (Egypt does not enforce renunciation requirement).

## Tax
- **Progressive income tax**: 0%–27.5%.
  - 0% up to EGP 40,000
  - 10% up to EGP 55,000
  - 15% up to EGP 70,000
  - 20% up to EGP 200,000
  - 22.5% up to EGP 400,000
  - 25% up to EGP 1,200,000
  - 27.5% above EGP 1,200,000
- **Foreign-source income**: Exempt for non-residents who do not maintain a domicile in Egypt. ^[https://uglobal.com/immigration/egypt]
- **Tax residency**: 183+ days/year or domicile/main place of business in Egypt.
- **Corporate tax**: 22.5% (general); higher for oil/gas.
- **VAT**: 14% (standard).
- **Capital gains**: 10% on real estate (special rules apply); no capital gains tax on Egyptian Exchange-listed securities.
- **No wealth tax.**
- **Inheritance tax**: applies.
- **Currency risk**: EGP has experienced significant devaluation. Foreign-currency income is advantageous; capital held in EGP loses value. Dollar-pegged thinking required for long-term planning.
- Double taxation treaties: ~55 treaties.

## IT / Digital Nomad
- **No dedicated digital nomad visa.**
- **De facto DN scene**: Small but growing in Cairo (Maadi, Zamalek, New Cairo), Hurghada, Dahab, and Sharm El-Sheikh. Tourist visa (30–90 days, visa-free/eVisa for many nationalities) is the primary mechanism.
- **Startup ecosystem**: Egypt has the largest tech startup scene in Africa/MENA — Flat6Labs Cairo, AUC Venture Lab, ITIDA (Information Technology Industry Development Agency) incentives. Cairo is a major tech hub.
- Internet: good in Cairo/Alexandria (fibre, 4G), slower in rural areas. Occasional restrictions on VoIP apps.
- Cost of living: very affordable — comfortable lifestyle at $800–1,500/mo.
- Legal status: Working remotely for foreign employer on tourist visa is common but technically an overstay risk after 90 days.
- ITIDA-registered companies may support residence for tech entrepreneurs/workers.

## Yacht / Maritime
- Extensive Red Sea and Mediterranean coastlines with world-class diving (Sharm El-Sheikh, Hurghada, Marsa Alam).
- Marinas: Porto Marina (North Coast), Hurghada Marina, Abu Tig Marina (El Gouna), Taba Heights Marina.
- Egypt-flag registration available.
- El Gouna is a well-developed yacht community with full-service marinas.
- Suez Canal transit is a key advantage for global cruising.
- Very affordable berthing and maintenance compared to Mediterranean Europe.

## Notes for RU citizens
- **Visa-free entry** for Russian passport holders (30 days). eVisa extendable to 90 days. One of the most accessible destinations for RU nationals.
- **Massive RU community** — Hurghada, Sharm El-Sheikh, and Cairo have significant Russian-speaking populations. Long-established tourist infrastructure catering to Russian market.
- **Banking**: Russian nationals can open bank accounts. Egyptian banks have become more cautious post-sanctions environment but remain accessible. US dollar accounts available.
- **Property ownership**: Russians are among the top foreign property buyers in Egypt — particularly Hurghada, Sharm, and North Coast. Prices are very low by global standards ($20,000–100,000 for apartments).
- **Direct flights**: Cairo–Moscow (EgyptAir, Aeroflot, daily), Hurghada–Moscow/SPB (multiple airlines, daily), Sharm El-Sheikh–Moscow (daily). One of the most connected MENA destinations to Russia.
- **CBI programme**: Open to Russian nationals. $250,000 donation is the cheapest CBI route in MENA. US E-2 eligibility through Egyptian passport is a potential indirect US pathway.
- **Currency risk**: Serious consideration. EGP devaluation means property "value" in hard currency terms fluctuates. Dollar-priced real estate developments exist in resort areas but not standard.
- **No sanctions**: Egypt maintains strong bilateral relationship with Russia (strategic partnership, nuclear plant, wheat trade).

## Community pulse
Egypt is the most accessible residency/CBI destination in MENA for Russian nationals. The CBI programme (from $250,000) is the cheapest in the region and includes routes that work for many profiles. The real estate residence route is accessible from ~$50,000. Quality of life in expat areas of Cairo, Hurghada, and El Gouna is good at low cost. Main drawbacks: weak passport (~49 visa-free), currency instability (EGP devaluation), bureaucracy, and infrastructure challenges outside premium zones. For those prioritizing affordability and access over passport strength, Egypt is a genuine contender.

## Country Card

Egypt is the Arab world's most populous country (~118 million) and its cultural heartland, straddling North Africa and the Middle East. The economy is large and diversified: Suez Canal revenues, tourism, agriculture, manufacturing, and remittances. The cost of living is among the lowest in the world, and Cairo is a megacity with a vibrant, chaotic energy. Citizenship is legally possible after 10 years of continuous residence but is rarely granted in practice. The Egyptian pound has undergone severe devaluation (2016, 2022–2024, 2025), creating currency instability. Digital nomads are drawn to low costs, Red Sea diving (Dahab, Sharm el-Sheikh), and year-round sunshine.

### Well-known strong points
- Extremely low cost of living — among the cheapest destinations globally
- Rich history and culture: pyramids, Luxor, Aswan, Islamic Cairo — unparalleled heritage tourism
- Large domestic market (~118M) with a young, growing population
- Year-round warm climate and Red Sea destinations popular with digital nomads
- Strategic location: Suez Canal, proximity to Europe/Gulf/Africa
- Large, English-speaking educated class concentrated in Cairo and Alexandria

### Well-known problems
- Citizenship rarely granted in practice — 10-year legal pathway is discretionary and slow
- Severe currency instability — EGP has devalued repeatedly; inflation is high (~30%+)
- Freedom House 18/100 — authoritarian regime, restricted press, widespread political repression
- Weak passport (49 visa-free) — limited global mobility
- Infrastructure strain: traffic, pollution, power cuts, and overcrowding in Cairo
- Bureaucratic complexity — starting a business or obtaining residency is paperwork-intensive
