## Overview
Israel's immigration system is unique: the [[law-of-return]] grants automatic citizenship to Jews and their descendants (children/grandchildren), while non-Jewish foreigners face a restrictive naturalization path. Israel has announced a Digital Nomad Visa ($5,000/mo, 12 months) and offers a B/5 Investor Visa for US citizens (reciprocal E-2). Israel does NOT have a citizenship-by-investment programme open to all nationalities. Personal income tax is progressive up to 50%. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_citizenship_law]

## Per-Program Detail

### B/5 Investor Visa
- **Official name**: B/5 Investor Visa (US-Israel E-2 reciprocal treaty)
- **Conditions**: Available ONLY to US citizens under the 2019 US-Israel E-2 treaty (reciprocal: Israelis get US E-2). Requires substantial investment in an active Israeli enterprise. Not open to other nationalities. ^[https://welcome-israel.com/israeli-citizenship/israeli-citizenship-by-investment]
- **Duration / renewal**: Typically initial up to 5 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Citizenship for B/5 holders generally requires separate naturalization (3 years out of 5 of residence + permanent residency + Hebrew proficiency).
- **Official source**: https://www.gov.il/en/departments/topics/visas_and_permits
- **Community verdict**: Functional but limited to US citizens; not a general investment residency.

### Digital Nomad Visa (Announced)
- **Official name**: Israel Digital Nomad Visa (announced, not yet fully implemented)
- **Conditions**: $5,000/mo minimum income from active remote work or business income (NOT passive income, pensions, or social security). Foreign-source income only. Open to all nationalities. No apostille required, no FBI background check, no medical exam, no mandatory local bank account, no consular interview. Bureaucracy rated low (1.0375/5). ^[https://rewireabroad.com/visas/israel-digital-nomad-visa]
- **Duration / renewal**: 12 months, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: No direct path. Does not count toward permanent residency. Must transition to another track for long-term status.
- **Official source**: Programme status: "announced" — procedures may shift.
- **Community verdict**: Announced but not yet operational — watch for full implementation details. High income threshold ($5K/mo).

### Work Visa (B/1)
- **Official name**: B/1 Work Visa
- **Conditions**: Employer sponsor, specialist skills not available locally. Labour market test. Valid for specific employer and role.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year initially, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: After 3 years out of 5 of residence + permanent residency (A/5) + Hebrew proficiency.
- **Official source**: https://www.gov.il/en/departments/topics/visas_and_permits
- **Community verdict**: Standard work permit, employer-dependent, bureaucratic.

### Startup/Innovation Visa
- **Official name**: Innovation Visas (various: "Innovation Visa", "Expert Visa" for tech)
- **Conditions**: Endorsement by Israel Innovation Authority or recognized tech ecosystem partner. For founders, entrepreneurs, and specialized tech workers. Israel's start-up visa ecosystem is evolving.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–3 years, renewable.
- **Official source**: https://innovationisrael.org.il
- **Community verdict**: Functional within tech ecosystem but fragmented.

### Student Residency
- **Official name**: A/2 Student Visa
- **Conditions**: Enrolment at recognized Israeli institution.
- **Duration / renewal**: Duration of programme.
- **Path to citizenship**: Time under A/2 does not typically count toward naturalization clock.
- **Official source**: https://www.gov.il
- **Community verdict**: Functional.

### Family Reunification
- **Official name**: Staged Family Unification Process (gradual process)
- **Conditions**: Spouse of Israeli citizen. Non-Jewish spouses undergo a 4.5-year staged process: temporary residence permits with gradually eased conditions, culminating in eligibility for citizenship after 4.5 years. Marriages must be valid under Israeli law. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_citizenship_law]
- **Duration / renewal**: 4.5-year process to citizenship.
- **Official source**: https://www.gov.il/en/departments/population_and_immigration_authority
- **Community verdict**: Lengthy, bureaucratic, requires genuine cohabitation proof.

## Citizenship
Israel offers citizenship through four main routes:
1. **Law of Return (1950)**: Automatic citizenship for Jews, children/grandchildren of Jews, and their spouses. Process via Aliyah (~6–18 months). Does not require prior residence. Dual citizenship permitted. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_citizenship_law]
2. **Naturalization**: Non-Jewish foreigners: 3 years out of 5 of legal residence while holding permanent residency (A/5) + Hebrew proficiency + renunciation of prior citizenship + intent to settle. 5-year waiting period after permanent residency before naturalization application approved. ^[https://lawoffice.org.il/en/moving-to-israel]
3. **Birth**: Born in Israel to at least one Israeli parent.
4. **By residence (historical)**: Pre-1948 residents and their descendants (largely Palestinian track — highly contested and limited in practice).

**No citizenship-by-investment programme exists.** The B/5 visa does not lead to citizenship directly. Israel is actively hostile to the concept of selling citizenship.

**Dual citizenship**: Permitted under Law of Return; not permitted for naturalized non-Jews (must renounce prior nationality, though enforcement varies).

## Tax
- **Progressive income tax**: 10%–50% (top rate). Marginal brackets:
  - 10% up to ILS 77,400/yr
  - 14%, 20%, 31%, 35%, 47% on intermediate brackets
  - 50% above ILS 721,560/yr
- **New immigrants (Olim)** benefit from 10-year tax holiday on foreign-source income (passive + active, depending on source).
- **Corporate tax**: 23%.
- **VAT**: 17% (standard), 0% on exports (including IT services).
- **Capital gains**: 25% (30% for substantial shareholders).
- **National Insurance (Bituach Leumi)**: up to 12% on employment income; 9.82%–17.83% for self-employed (including health tax).
- **No wealth tax, no inheritance tax.**

## IT / Digital Nomad
- **Digital Nomad Visa** (announced): $5,000/mo income, 12 months renewable. Does not lead to PR.
- Israel is "Startup Nation" — one of the world's highest densities of tech startups, venture capital per capita, and R&D expenditure.
- Tel Aviv is a global tech hub with a vibrant startup ecosystem.
- However: high cost of living (especially Tel Aviv), high income tax (up to 50%), and complex bureaucracy offset some advantages.
- New immigrants (Olim) receive significant tech ecosystem integration support and tax benefits.
- For non-Jewish digital nomads: DN visa (when operational) is the primary option; otherwise B/1 work visa or tourist visa (B/2, 90 days visa-free for many Western nationals).

## Yacht / Maritime
- Extensive Mediterranean coastline (Tel Aviv, Herzliya, Haifa, Ashdod).
- Herzliya Marina — Israel's premier yachting marina.
- Israel-flag vessel registration available.
- Maritime industry is primarily commercial/defence; leisure yachting is smaller than Mediterranean neighbours (Greece, Cyprus, Turkey).

## Notes for RU citizens
- **Law of Return eligible**: ~900,000+ Russian Jews and their descendants can obtain Israeli citizenship through Aliyah — this is the single largest pathway for RU nationals globally. No investment required, citizenship within ~6–18 months. ^[https://welcome-israel.com/israeli-citizenship]
- **Non-Jewish RU citizens**: Standard naturalization or work/family routes apply. B/2 tourist visa (90 days, eVisa required — not visa-free for RU).
- **Sanctions environment**: Complex. Israel maintains a neutral/balancing position. RU nationals can access Israeli banking but enhanced KYC applies. No EU/US-style sanctions.
- **Massive Russian-speaking community**: ~1.2M Russian speakers in Israel (~15% of population). Russian is widely spoken, media available.
- **Direct flights**: Tel Aviv–Moscow (multiple daily via El Al, Red Wings, Azimuth). Busiest RU-Israel air corridor globally.
- **Dual citizenship permitted** for Law of Return immigrants; tax benefits for first 10 years on foreign income.
- Israeli passport: visa-free access to ~160+ countries, including Schengen area, UK, Canada (ETA). Very strong passport.

## Community pulse
Israel is fundamentally a Jewish immigration country (Law of Return). For eligible individuals, the Aliyah pathway is unmatched: automatic citizenship, dual nationality, 10-year tax holiday, strong passport. For non-Jewish immigrants, it's a standard developed-country system: work visas, naturalization after 3+ years, high tax, high cost of living. The announced DN visa may improve access for non-Jewish remote workers. Israeli bureaucracy is notoriously complex. The security situation and regional tensions are a consideration for potential residents.

## Country Card

Israel is a developed, high-income democracy with the strongest tech ecosystem in the Middle East ("Startup Nation"). The economy is knowledge-driven, anchored by cybersecurity, AI, fintech, and biotech. Citizenship is accessible to Jews under the Law of Return and possible (though lengthy) for others through naturalization. The cost of living is the highest in the region, comparable to Western Europe, especially in Tel Aviv. Israel's passport is among the world's strongest (166 visa-free). The country combines Mediterranean lifestyle with deep historical/religious significance, drawing tourism and business interest globally.

### Well-known strong points
- World-class tech ecosystem: highest startups per capita globally; major R&D hub
- Strong passport (166 visa-free) with visa-free access to US, EU, UK
- Democratic institutions with independent judiciary and free press (FH 73/100)
- Citizenship pathway exists — Law of Return for Jews; naturalization possible for others
- Mediterranean lifestyle: beaches, culinary scene, vibrant Tel Aviv culture
- Excellent healthcare and education systems

### Well-known problems
- Very high cost of living — Tel Aviv consistently ranks among world's most expensive cities
- High personal taxation (up to 50% top bracket) — significant burden for remote workers
- Ongoing security situation and regional instability affect daily life
- Citizenship naturalization for non-Jews is slow, bureaucratic, and discretionary
- Housing crisis — severe shortage and high prices in central areas
- Political polarization and periodic social unrest
