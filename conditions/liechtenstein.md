## Overview
Liechtenstein is a doubly landlocked principality between Switzerland and Austria, an EEA (not EU) member with the Swiss franc as its currency. Its residence system is among the most restrictive in Europe: a tiny annual quota (72 EEA permits, 17 Swiss, negligible third-country numbers) and no meaningful investment-residency route. The draw is a **territorial tax system** — foreign-source income is generally not taxed — plus a top effective income-tax rate of ~22.4%. Citizenship takes **30 years**. ^[https://www.llv.li/en/national-administration/migration-and-passport-office/residence-permits/residence-permit]

## Per-Program Detail

### Passive residence
- **Official name**: Residence in Liechtenstein without gainful employment
- **Conditions**: Persons of independent financial means; must prove substantial wealth; subject to a minuscule, discretionary quota. In practice "not a viable option for virtually anyone" — highly opaque. ^[https://rewireabroad.com/country/liechtenstein]
- **Duration / renewal**: Permit B (annual/renewable), then settlement.
- **Path to citizenship**: 30 years residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.llv.li/en/national-administration/migration-and-passport-office/residence-permits/residence-permit
- **Community verdict**: Problematic — effectively closed to most applicants.

### Employment residence
- **Official name**: Residence for gainful employment (quota system)
- **Conditions**: Employer must prove no suitable Liechtenstein/EEA candidate; annual quota: 56 EEA work permits + 16 other EEA, 17 Swiss. Cross-border workers (Grenzgänger) commute from Austria/Switzerland and are exempt from the residence quota. ^[https://movetoeu.eu/guides/liechtenstein/]
- **Duration / renewal**: Permit B/L; permanent residence after 5 years continuous lawful residence.
- **Path to citizenship**: 30 years residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.llv.li
- **Community verdict**: Works well only for highly specialised hires at major employers (Hilti, LGT, Ivoclar); most workers live across the border.

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: **30 years** of residence; years 10–20 count double; reduced to **10 years** if the applicant's commune votes to approve, or **5 years** via marriage to a citizen. ^[https://rewireabroad.com/country/liechtenstein]
- **Language**: German.
- **Approval**: municipal vote + parliament + Princely assent.
- **Dual citizenship**: NOT permitted — must renounce prior nationality.

## Tax
- **Territorial system**: foreign-source income generally not taxed — a major advantage for internationally mobile residents. ^[https://taxratesbycountry.com/tax-rates-in-liechtenstein]
- **Income tax (Erwerbssteuer)**: national 1%–8%, plus municipal surcharge 150–200% → effective top rate ~**22.4%**. ^[https://taxatlas.io/country/liechtenstein]
- **Corporate tax**: flat **12.5%**.
- **Wealth tax**: 0.06%–0.89% of net wealth (combined income+wealth capped).
- **No** capital-gains tax on movable assets (securities, crypto for individuals); Swiss **VAT 8.1%**.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa; independent remote work is not a recognised category. The territorial tax regime is theoretically attractive to nomads, but there is no lawful way for a non-EEA remote worker to settle. IT employment is possible only through the EEA quota for specialists at Liechtenstein firms.

## Yacht / Maritime
Landlocked (doubly landlocked) — no coastline or yachting. Not applicable.

## Notes for RU citizens
No formal ban, but third-country residence permits are exceptionally rare. Russian nationals must pass the same discretionary quota as all non-EEA applicants; in practice a viable route does not exist without an employer sponsor. Banking in Liechtenstein (a major wealth-management centre) historically served Russian HNWIs but has tightened KYC sharply since 2022.

## Community pulse
Forums consistently rate Liechtenstein as one of the hardest places to obtain residence or citizenship. The only realistic paths are employment at a major local employer or marriage. The 30-year citizenship clock and communal-vote requirement are repeatedly cited as the most extreme in Europe.

## Country Card

### General description
Liechtenstein is a 160 km² Alpine principality of ~40,000 people, economically dominated by manufacturing (Hilti, Ivoclar), financial services, and wealth management, using the Swiss franc. As an EEA member it is open to Swiss/EEA workers under a strict quota, but third-country immigration is near-impossible. Its territorial tax system and strong institutions make it attractive in principle, but residence is the bottleneck.

### Well-known strong points
- Territorial taxation — foreign income not taxed
- Strong passport (181 visa-free, Henley 2026); EEA access
- Very high income levels and political stability (FH 90/100)
- Low crime, excellent Alpine quality of life
- Robust financial/wealth-management centre

### Well-known problems
- One of the world's most restrictive residence systems (tiny quotas)
- 30-year citizenship with communal vote and renunciation of prior nationality
- No investment or digital-nomad residence route
- Very high cost of living
- No coastline; small and opaque for outsiders
