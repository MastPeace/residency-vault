## Overview
Guyana is South America's only English-speaking country and a CARICOM member, undergoing a historic oil-driven boom (one of the world's fastest-growing economies). Naturalisation requires roughly **6 years** of residence (12 months continuous immediately before applying, plus 5 years aggregate across the prior 7-year window). Dual citizenship is permitted. There is no citizenship- or residence-by-investment programme and no digital-nomad visa. ^[https://legalclarity.org/guyanese-citizenship-by-naturalization-requirements-and-process]

## Per-Program Detail

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Work permit
- **Conditions**: Binding job offer from a Guyanese employer; employer sponsorship. Oil-sector and services demand has risen sharply with the boom.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Naturalisation after ~6 years' residence (12 months continuous + 5 years aggregate over 7 years); Commonwealth residence may count toward the aggregate. ^[https://legalclarity.org/guyanese-citizenship-by-naturalization-requirements-and-process]
- **Official source**: https://mola.gov.gy/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — oil-sector hiring active, but work permits and administration remain under-resourced.

## Citizenship
- **Naturalisation**: 12 months continuous residence immediately before application, plus at least 5 years aggregate residence in the 7 years prior (minimum ~6 years from arrival). Good character required; no statutory language or civics test (English is official). ^[https://legalclarity.org/guyanese-citizenship-by-naturalization-requirements-and-process]
- **Registration**: Spouse of a Guyanese citizen may register (marriage route). ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyanese_nationality_law]
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted (President, MPs and certain offices restricted to sole citizens). ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyanese_nationality_law]
- **Passport**: ~88 visa-free/on-arrival destinations (CARICOM + Schengen for short stays).

## Tax
- Residence-based taxation; tax resident if >183 days in a year. Personal income tax progressive up to ~28%; corporate income tax 25%; VAT 14%. Oil-revenue windfall has not yet produced a non-dom regime.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa. Georgetown is a growing business hub driven by the oil sector, but tech/nomad infrastructure is still thin. Remote workers enter as tourists or via employer sponsorship.

## Yacht / Maritime
Atlantic/Caribbean coastline with the Demerara River port at Georgetown. Limited leisure-yachting infrastructure; not a yachting destination, though Caribbean proximity is a plus.

## Notes for RU citizens
- Visa required; English working language (major advantage).
- No residence-by-investment; residence is employer-driven (oil/services).
- Dual citizenship permitted; a Guyanese passport offers CARICOM free movement.

## Community Pulse
Rising interest driven by the oil boom (foreign workers, services), but no investor-residence or nomad community. The public debate on a possible CBI programme has not resulted in legislation. Administration is under-resourced relative to demand.

## Country Card

### General description
Guyana is a small, English-speaking South American nation and CARICOM member on the Atlantic/Caribbean coast, now one of the world's fastest-growing economies due to offshore oil. It offers a relatively accessible ~6-year path to citizenship with dual nationality permitted, but no investment-residence, CBI, or nomad visa. Its key appeal is English-language administration and CARICOM mobility, plus oil-sector opportunities.

### Well-known strong points
- English-speaking (only such country in South America); CARICOM free movement
- Dual citizenship permitted; ~6-year naturalisation window
- Fast-growing economy; rising job opportunities (oil/services)
- Democratic and "Free" (FH 74/100)
- Caribbean coastal location

### Well-known problems
- No residence-by-investment, CBI, or nomad visa
- Small, under-resourced administration relative to demand
- Rising cost of living driven by the oil boom
- Border disputes (Venezuela over Essequibo) add regional risk
- Under-developed infrastructure

## Status
- **Last monitored**: 2026-10-11
- **Recent changes**: No immigration-policy changes confirmed; CBI discussed but not legislated.
- **Watch items**: Possible CBI introduction; work-permit capacity; border-tension spillover.
