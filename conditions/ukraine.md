## Overview
Ukraine is the largest country entirely in Europe, now under martial law since the February 2022 Russian invasion. Its residence system is employment-driven (no golden visa or digital-nomad visa), but its low flat taxes — especially the 5% single tax for IT sole traders (FOP) and the Diia City regime — make it a major destination for foreign and returning IT workers. Citizenship requires 5 years of residence plus a Ukrainian-language and Constitution/history exam. ^[https://dmsu.gov.ua/en-home/services/acquisition-of-ukrainian-citizenship/acquiring-ukrainian-citizenship-by-admission.html]

## Per-Program Detail

### Permanent residence
- **Official name**: Посвідка на постійне проживання (permanent residence permit / immigration permit)
- **Conditions**: Issued within an immigration quota; investment of ≥ **$100,000** in the Ukrainian economy qualifies, as do highly-qualified specialists and relatives of citizens (Law "On Immigration"). ^[https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/anot/en/2491-14]
- **Duration / renewal**: Permanent (biometric card issued for 10 years, status indefinite).
- **Path to citizenship**: Counts toward the 5-year naturalisation clock.
- **Official source**: https://dmsu.gov.ua
- **Community verdict**: Works well but quota-limited and administratively slow.

### Employment residence
- **Official name**: Посвідка на тимчасове проживання (temporary residence permit)
- **Conditions**: Work permit + employment contract with a Ukrainian employer; IT specialists can use the Diia City special contract or register as a ФОП (sole trader) to work with foreign clients.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–3 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years continuous residence (with immigration permit).
- **Official source**: https://dmsu.gov.ua
- **Community verdict**: Works well — widely used by foreign IT workers; processing slowed by martial law.

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: 5 years continuous legal residence + immigration permit. ^[https://dmsu.gov.ua/en-home/services/acquisition-of-ukrainian-citizenship/acquiring-ukrainian-citizenship-by-admission.html]
- **Language**: Ukrainian (B1); Constitution & history exam (45 questions, mandatory since 2023). ^[https://www.kyivpost.com/post/74121]
- **Dual citizenship**: multiple citizenship legalised in June 2025 (previously nominally single). ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_nationality_law]

## Tax
- **Employees**: 18% personal income tax + 5% military levy (raised from 1.5% on 1 Jan 2025) ≈ **23% combined**. ^[https://tax.gov.ua/en/new-about-taxes--news-/858588.html]
- **IT ФОП (sole trader, Group 3)**: 5% single tax on turnover + 1% military levy + fixed social contribution (~UAH 1,900/mo). ^[https://employsome.com/hire/ukraine/fop-ukraine]
- **Diia City IT specialists**: 5% PIT + 5% military levy on special-contract remuneration.
- **Corporate**: 18% standard (25% for some); VAT 20%.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa, but Ukraine is a top IT-outsourcing hub (second-largest in Europe pre-war). Remote workers enter via ФОП registration or Diia City employment; Ukraine also runs a "uResidency" (e-Residency) for foreign entrepreneurs to run a ФОП remotely. Martial law means remote work is geographically flexible but infrastructure is under pressure.

## Yacht / Maritime
Extensive Black Sea coastline and major ports (Odesa, Mykolaiv, Chornomorsk), but maritime activity is severely curtailed by the war and a Russian naval blockade of export shipping (the Black Sea Grain Initiative remains intermittently suspended). Yacht cruising is effectively non-operational during hostilities.

## Notes for RU citizens
Temporary protection applies to Ukrainians, not Russians. Russian nationals face entry restrictions and can hold a temporary residence permit only on limited grounds (family, work with approval). A small IT-driven Russian-speaking community existed pre-2022 but has largely relocated. Ukraine does not generally naturalise Russian citizens during martial law without exceptional grounds.

## Community pulse
Foreign IT workers praise the 5% ФОП tax and low cost of living, but the war dominates all relocation decisions: power outages, mobilisation risks, and banking instability are the main deterrents. Processing of permits continues but is slower and less predictable under martial law.

## Country Card

### General description
Ukraine is a large Eastern European country with rich agricultural and IT sectors, currently fighting a full-scale war following Russia's 2022 invasion. Its flat, low taxes and deep technical talent pool made it Europe's second-largest IT-outsourcing destination. For residents, the attractions are the 5% ФОП single tax, low cost of living, and a relatively short 5-year citizenship path — all now shadowed by war risk, power instability, and a depopulating, mobilisation-era labour market.

### Well-known strong points
- Very low taxes: 5% single tax for IT sole traders; 18%+5% for employees
- Low cost of living (Numbeo ~30)
- Large, highly skilled IT workforce and outsourcing ecosystem
- Short 5-year path to citizenship (with exam)
- Multiple citizenship legalised (June 2025)

### Well-known problems
- Full-scale war since 2022: security, power outages, mobilisation risk
- No golden visa, DN visa, or passive-income residence
- Wartime restrictions and slower, less predictable permit processing
- Corruption and weak rule of law (WJP rank 88/142)
- Economy contracted sharply; salary levels low by EU standards

## Status
- **Last monitored**: 2026-10-26

- **Watch items**: Citizenship-test reform; martial-law extension; further tax changes (military levy).
