## Overview
Senegal is a stable West African democracy with a French-influenced immigration system. Foreign nationals require a Permis de Travail (Work Permit) from the Direction de l'Emploi and a Titre de Séjour (Residence Card) from the Direction de la Police des Étrangers (DPETV). There is no dedicated digital nomad visa, investment residency scheme, or retirement visa. A digital nomad visa is reportedly in development. Citizenship requires 10 years continuous residence. Dual citizenship is permitted. ^[https://sn.usembassy.gov/how-to-obtain-a-residence-permit-in-senegal]

## Per-Program Detail

### employment_residency — Permis de Travail + Titre de Séjour
- **Official name**: Permis de Travail (Work Permit) + Titre de Séjour (Residence Card)
- **Conditions**: Employment contract legalised by Inspection du Travail (Labour Inspection). Visa de Long Séjour obtained at Senegalese embassy abroad. Medical certificate from local GP (XOF 20,000). Criminal record from home country OR Attestation de Casier Judiciaire from consulate (USD 50). Repatriation bond deposit (XOF 130,000 for US citizens). Proof of accommodation. Birth certificate. Application fee: XOF 15,000 stamp. ^[https://sn.usembassy.gov/how-to-obtain-a-residence-permit-in-senegal]
- **Duration**: 1–2 years, renewable. Temporary residency issued in 2–3 months; actual card takes 1–1.5 years (renew interim paper every 6 months). ^[https://sn.usembassy.gov/how-to-obtain-a-residence-permit-in-senegal]
- **Path to citizenship**: Permanent residence after 5 years of uninterrupted legal stay. Citizenship: 10 years continuous lawful residence. ^[https://remotepeople.com/countries/senegal/employer-of-record/work-visa-permit]
- **Official source**: https://sn.usembassy.gov/how-to-obtain-a-residence-permit-in-senegal (US Embassy guidance — most practical English-language source); DPETV directly for French speakers.
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — process is bureaucratic and slow (card takes up to 1.5 years), but functional. French language ability is essential for navigating the administration.

## Citizenship
- Naturalisation: 10 years continuous lawful residence. ^[https://remotepeople.com/countries/senegal/employer-of-record/work-visa-permit]
- Marriage to Senegalese citizen: 5 years (reduced timeline).
- Language: French (official); Wolof widely spoken. French proficiency expected.
- Dual citizenship: Permitted. Senegal allows dual nationality. ^[https://www.multiplecitizenship.com/wscl/ws_SENEGAL.html]
- Passport: visa-free to ~55 destinations. ECOWAS free movement.
- **Honest assessment**: The 10-year timeline is long but not extreme. Dual citizenship permitted is a positive. Processing is bureaucratic but not impossible. Senegal is not a passport-shopping destination.

## Tax
- Territorial tax system for non-residents. Tax residents taxed on worldwide income.
- Personal income tax: progressive 0%–40% (top rate on XOF 14M+/yr, ~USD 23,000).
- Corporate tax: 30% standard.
- VAT: 18%.
- Social contributions: IPRES (pension) + CSS (health).

## IT / Digital Nomad
No dedicated DN visa as of Sept 2026, but Senegal is "actively preparing" one per EY Global Immigration Index 2025. Dakar is a growing tech hub with decent internet (4G/LTE, fibre in Plateau/Almadies), co-working spaces (Jokalante, Impact Hub Dakar), and a vibrant startup scene. Remote workers currently rely on 90-day visa-free entry for many nationalities (extendable) — this is a legal grey area. Cost of living: moderate.

## Yacht / Maritime
Senegal has an Atlantic coastline (Dakar, Saly). Dakar is a major West African port. The Dakar Yacht Club exists. Limited superyacht infrastructure compared to Cape Town or Mauritius. Strategic mid-Atlantic location.

## Notes for RU citizens
Visa-free or visa on arrival (varies — check with embassy). French proficiency is important for daily life in Dakar. Senegal maintains neutral international relations. Small Russian-speaking community. Banking: functional, CFA franc zone provides currency stability. The reported development of a DN visa is worth monitoring.
