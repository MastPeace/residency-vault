## Overview
The Philippines offers two flagship residence programs: the Special Resident Retiree's Visa (SRRV, via the Philippine Retirement Authority) and the Special Investor's Resident Visa (SIRV, US$75,000). Standard work (9g), student (9f), and spouse (13a) visas complete the menu. Naturalization requires 10 years of continuous residence (5 with qualifying conditions); dual citizenship is available to former natural-born Filipinos (RA 9225). ^[https://www.pra.gov.ph/]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Special Investor's Resident Visa (SIRV)
- **Conditions**: US$75,000 investment in a registered Philippine enterprise (or approved investment). ^[https://www.immigration.gov.ph/]
- **Duration / renewal**: Indefinite (as long as investment is maintained).
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years continuous residence (5 with qualifications).
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.ph/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — viable but the investment must remain in place; less popular than SRRV.

### highly_skilled_employment
- **Official name**: 9(g) Pre-arranged Employment Visa
- **Conditions**: Job offer; employer obtains Alien Employment Permit (AEP) demonstrating no qualified Filipino available.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–3 years.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.ph/
- **Community verdict**: Works well for executive/specialist roles.

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: 9(g) Work Visa
- **Conditions**: Job offer + AEP.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–3 years.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.ph/
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### student_residency
- **Official name**: 9(f) Student Visa
- **Conditions**: Enrollment in recognized institution; proof of support.
- **Duration / renewal**: Course duration.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.ph/
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: 13(a) (spouse of Filipino) / dependent visas
- **Conditions**: Marriage to a Filipino citizen, or dependent of a resident visa holder.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year probationary, then permanent.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.ph/
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Special Resident Retiree's Visa (SRRV) — SRRV Classic / Smile / Courtesy
- **Conditions**: Principal age 40+ (50+ for Classic); SRRV Classic deposit US$15,000 (50+, with pension ≥US$800/mo) up to US$50,000 (40–49, no pension); Smile US$20,000 (35+). ^[https://www.pra.gov.ph/SRRVisa]
- **Duration / renewal**: Indefinite, multiple-entry, as long as deposit maintained.
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years continuous residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.pra.gov.ph/
- **Community verdict**: Works well — one of the world's most affordable retiree visas.

### citizenship_by_investment
Not available.

## Citizenship
- **Naturalization**: 10 years continuous residence (5 years with qualifying conditions such as marriage to a Filipino or notable contribution); good moral character; ability to speak a Philippine language. ^[https://www.immigration.gov.ph/]
- **Dual citizenship**: Available to former natural-born Filipinos who re-acquire under RA 9225 (no residence requirement).
- **Practical reality**: Judicial naturalization is slow and rare; most expats remain on SRRV/13a indefinitely.

## Tax
- **Individual income tax**: Progressive 0%–35% (top rate on income > PHP 8,000,000). ^[https://www.bir.gov.ph/]
- **Residency**: Resident aliens taxed on Philippine-source income only (not worldwide) — a significant advantage.
- **No special nomad regime.**

## IT / Digital Nomad
No dedicated digital-nomad visa. Remote workers for foreign employers often stay on tourist extensions or SRRV; working locally without a permit is a grey area. Internet is improving but variable outside metro Manila/Cebu. Growing nomad scene in Siargao, Cebu, and BGC (Manila).

## Yacht / Maritime
Vast archipelago (~7,600 islands). Growing yachting/cruising: Subic Bay, Puerto Galera, Coron/El Nido (Palawan). Marina infrastructure is developing; foreign-flagged yachts can obtain a temporary import permit. Not yet a top-tier yachting hub.

## Notes for RU Citizens
Visa-free entry for Russian nationals (up to 30 days, extendable). SRRV/SIRV open to RU applicants. Banking for RU nationals is possible but compliance-heavy. Small but growing Russian-speaking community in Manila and Cebu.

## Community Pulse
The SRRV is the flagship — cheap, indefinite, and popular with Western and Asian retirees. SIRV is less used. Main complaints: BI bureaucracy, slow judicial naturalization, and variable internet. The Philippines is seen as an affordable, English-speaking, friendly base rather than a citizenship play.

## Country Card

### General description
The Philippines is a Southeast Asian archipelago of ~7,600 islands with a young, English-speaking population and a service- and remittance-driven economy. It offers one of the world's cheapest indefinite retiree visas (SRRV, from US$10,000–15,000 deposit) and an investor visa (SIRV, US$75,000), making it a popular low-cost long-stay base, though it is not a strong citizenship or tax-optimization play.

### Well-known strong points
- Very affordable retiree (SRRV) and investor (SIRV) visas
- English is an official language — minimal language barrier
- Resident aliens taxed only on Philippine-source income
- Low cost of living and friendly, welcoming culture
- Large, dispersed expat community and many islands

### Well-known problems
- No digital-nomad visa; weak IT infrastructure outside major cities
- Slow, bureaucratic immigration and judicial systems
- Rule of law and corruption concerns (WJP ~0.46)
- Naturalization slow and rare; dual citizenship only for former Filipinos
- Typhoons and seismic risk
