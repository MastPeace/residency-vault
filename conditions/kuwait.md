## Overview
Kuwait launched a new 15-year investor residency programme in June 2026 (Cabinet Resolution No. 651/2026), offering long-term residence to qualifying foreign investors through the Kuwait Direct Investment Promotion Authority (KDIPA). Personal income tax is 0%. Kuwait does NOT offer citizenship to expatriates. ^[https://kuwaittimes.com/article/44928/kuwait/new-regulations-for-foreign-investor-residency-permits-up-to-15-years]

## Per-Program Detail

### Investor Residency (KDIPA)
- **Official name**: إقامة المستثمر (Investor Residency) under Law No. 116 of 2013
- **Conditions**:
  - Investment entity must hold a valid KDIPA investment license.
  - Minimum investment volume: KD 5,000,000 (~$16.3M). ^[https://kuwaittimes.com/article/44928/kuwait/new-regulations-for-foreign-investor-residency-permits-up-to-15-years]
  - Minimum capital: KD 1,000,000 (~$3.26M) with proof of deposit inside Kuwait.
  - Actual operating premises in Kuwait.
  - Compliance with Kuwaiti national employment quotas (set by KDIPA).
  - Clean criminal record, valid passport (6+ months), undertaking by entity accepting legal responsibility for documents.
  - Eligible: owners of the investment entity, partners, directors, senior management, and immediate family (spouse, parents, children).
- **Duration / renewal**: Up to 15 years. Renewal: apply 60 days before expiry, demonstrate ongoing compliance.
- **Path to citizenship**: None.
- **Official source**: https://www.moi.gov.kw, KDIPA via Law No. 116/2013
- **Community verdict**: New programme (Jun 2026) — no established track record. High threshold limits accessibility.

### Employment Residency
- **Official name**: Standard Work Permit (Iqama)
- **Conditions**: Employer sponsor, valid labour contract. Medical exam, biometrics. Minimum wage: varies by sector and nationality. Employer-dependent (kafeel system).
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–2 years, renewable. Tied to employer.
- **Path to citizenship**: None.
- **Official source**: https://www.moi.gov.kw
- **Community verdict**: Standard GCC kafeel system.

### Student Residency
- **Official name**: Student Visa
- **Conditions**: Enrolment at accredited Kuwaiti institution.
- **Duration / renewal**: Duration of study.
- **Path to citizenship**: None.
- **Official source**: https://www.moi.gov.kw
- **Community verdict**: Functional.

### Family Reunification
- **Official name**: Family Joining Visa
- **Conditions**: Sponsor must hold valid Iqama, meet minimum salary (typically KD 450+/mo). Covers spouse and children.
- **Duration / renewal**: Tied to sponsor's Iqama.
- **Path to citizenship**: None.
- **Official source**: https://www.moi.gov.kw
- **Community verdict**: Standard, salary threshold may exclude lower-income workers.

## Citizenship
**Not available for expatriates.** Kuwaiti citizenship is by descent (Kuwaiti father). Naturalization is limited to: Arab nationals with 15+ years residence (reduced from 20 years), Arabic fluency, and Muslim faith; or women married to Kuwaiti citizens (after 5–15 years, discretionary). Approvals are rare — typically a few dozen per year. No citizenship-by-investment programme. Stateless Bidoon population remains a major unresolved issue.

## Tax
- **0% personal income tax** — no tax on salary, dividends, or capital gains.
- **15% corporate income tax** on foreign-owned businesses.
- **0% VAT** (not implemented as of 2026, though GCC framework agreement exists).
- **No wealth tax, no inheritance tax.**
- **Zakat**: 1% on Kuwaiti/GCC-owned companies; 2.5% on individual Muslims (religious, not state-enforced for individuals).
- Social insurance: 11.5% employer contribution for Kuwaiti employees in private sector (expat workers: indemnity system instead of pension).

## IT / Digital Nomad
- **No dedicated digital nomad visa.** Kuwait does not offer a remote work visa.
- IT sector is developing (Kuwait Vision 2035) but remains oil-dominated.
- Internet infrastructure: good (fibre, 4G/5G), but online censorship exists.
- For remote workers: tourist visa (90 days for many nationalities, including Russia) is the primary option.
- No clear pathway for long-term digital nomads — investor residency threshold is too high.

## Yacht / Maritime
- Kuwait has a modest yachting scene (Kuwait Yacht Club, Marina Crescent).
- Kuwait-flag registration available.
- Persian Gulf access. Less developed than UAE/Qatar/Oman for leisure maritime.
- Private yacht ownership possible but no significant tax advantages or dedicated infrastructure.

## Notes for RU citizens
- **Visa-free entry** (90 days) for Russian passport holders — one of the few GCC states with this for RU.
- **Very small RU community** — much smaller than UAE.
- **No sanctions**, Kuwaiti banks generally accessible but conservative.
- **Limited appeal** for RU migrants compared to UAE: no Golden Visa equivalent, investor threshold very high (KD 5M), restrictive social environment, limited entertainment options.
- **Direct flights**: Kuwait City–Moscow via Jazeera Airways and Kuwait Airways (3–4 weekly).
- Kuwait has maintained neutral diplomatic stance toward Russia.

## Community pulse
Kuwait has the smallest expat appeal among GCC states for lifestyle/residency seekers. The new 15-year investor visa is a step forward but the KD 5M threshold makes it accessible only to major corporate investors. For individual HNWI/residency seekers, UAE, Qatar, and Bahrain are far more practical. Quality of life is lower than neighbours; bureaucratic inertia and restrictive social environment are common complaints. Cost of living is moderate.

## Country Card

Kuwait is a high-income oil state with the Gulf's most active parliament and some of the world's largest sovereign wealth reserves. Personal income tax is 0%, and the cost of living is moderate. The 2026 Cabinet Resolution No. 651 introduced a new 15-year investor residency programme via KDIPA with a KD 5 million threshold. Expatriates make up approximately two-thirds of the population, mostly in employment-based visas. Kuwait has historically had the most politically engaged citizenry in the Gulf, though Freedom House rates it Not Free (31/100), declining from Partly Free status in 2024.

### Well-known strong points
- 0% personal income tax backed by substantial oil wealth
- Moderate cost of living — cheaper than UAE/Qatar for housing and daily expenses
- Active parliament with more political debate than any other Gulf monarchy
- Strong Kuwaiti dinar — one of the world's highest-valued currencies
- Well-established banking sector and deep capital markets
- Generous state welfare for Kuwaiti citizens; subsidized fuel, electricity, and healthcare

### Well-known problems
- No pathway to citizenship — restricted to descendants of pre-1920 residents; almost never granted
- Very limited self-sponsored residency — investor visa requires KD 5M (2026)
- Political gridlock between parliament and government causes policy stagnation
- Bureaucracy is notoriously slow and paper-heavy; wasta (connections) culture pervasive
- Declining civil liberties: Freedom House score dropped from 38 to 31 (2024→2025)
- Extreme summer heat (May–September) with dust storms; limited outdoor activity
