## Overview
Lebanon has one of the easiest residence pathways in the Middle East — foreign nationals can obtain residence via employment, business, or property ownership — but its 2019 financial collapse, banking crisis, and a near-total freeze on naturalisation make it a residence-only destination with a weak passport (~42 visa-free). Income tax is progressive 2–25% (revalued post-crisis), and the currency is severely devalued. ^[https://2001-2009.state.gov/e/eeb/ifd/2008/100903.htm]

## Per-Program Detail

### Real-estate residence
- **Official name**: Residence based on real-estate ownership
- **Conditions**: Foreigners may own up to 3,000 m² without a permit (more with Cabinet approval); property ownership supports a residence application. Cumulative foreign ownership is capped at 3% of land per district (10% in Beirut). ^[https://2001-2009.state.gov/e/eeb/ifd/2008/100903.htm]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–3 years, renewable; permanent residence possible after continuous residence (exact terms vary by administration).
- **Path to citizenship**: Naturalisation is discretionary and effectively frozen.
- **Official source**: https://www.general-security.gov.lb
- **Community verdict**: Works well on paper — the cheapest "clear pathway" in the region — but undermined by the financial crisis.

### Business residence
- **Official name**: Business ("Employer") work permit + residence
- **Conditions**: Investor's capital share ≥ **$67,000** and a pledge to hire three Lebanese nationals registered with the National Social Security Fund within six months. ^[https://2001-2009.state.gov/e/eeb/ifd/2008/100903.htm]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–3 years.
- **Path to citizenship**: Discretionary.
- **Official source**: https://www.general-security.gov.lb
- **Community verdict**: Mixed — simple threshold but hiring obligations and the crisis deter investors.

## Citizenship
- **Naturalisation**: Extremely rare and discretionary; effectively frozen since the civil war. No investment amount unlocks citizenship, and there is no clear residence-to-citizenship pathway. ^[https://freedomhouse.org/country/lebanon/freedom-world/2025]
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted in practice.

## Tax
- **Personal income tax**: progressive **2%–25%** (salary brackets revalued after the currency collapse; effective rate at average income ~11%). ^[https://lb.icalculator.com/income-tax-rates/2025.html]
- **Corporate tax**: 17% standard.
- **VAT**: 11%; capital gains 15%; dividends 10%.
- No wealth tax. Income tax is levied in the collapsed Lebanese pound, making effective USD burdens low but administration chaotic. ^[https://lifeindexed.com/tax/middle-east/lebanon?year=2025]

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa. Lebanon has a small but resilient freelance/tech community, but the banking crisis, capital controls, and unreliable power/internet make it unsuitable for most remote workers. Currency instability is the dominant practical obstacle.

## Yacht / Maritime
Long Mediterranean coastline and a historic maritime culture (Beirut, Jounieh, Byblos marinas), but the economic collapse has degraded infrastructure. Yacht registration exists but is rarely used by foreign owners compared to [[uae]] or [[malta]].

## Notes for RU citizens
No specific restriction on Russian nationals. Lebanon has long been a Russian-tourist and expat destination. The practical barriers are banking (foreign transfers difficult), the devalued currency, and infrastructure failures — not nationality policy.

## Community pulse
Expat and diaspora forums describe Lebanon as cheap and socially welcoming but operationally broken: unreliable electricity, a collapsed banking system, and cash-based daily life. Residence is easy to obtain but offers little utility; few international relocators choose it now, and the diaspora continues to leave.

## Country Card

### General description
Lebanon is a small Mediterranean country with a multi-confessional political system, historically a banking and services hub for the Levant. Since the 2019 financial collapse and 2020 Beirut port explosion, the economy, currency, and public services have largely broken down. Residence is comparatively easy to obtain (property, business, or employment), but the passport is weak and naturalisation is effectively frozen, leaving Lebanon a low-cost but low-utility base.

### Well-known strong points
- Easy, low-cost residence pathways (property/business/employment)
- Mediterranean coastline and mild climate (~2,900 sunshine hours)
- Cheap cost of living in hard-currency terms after devaluation
- Welcoming, cosmopolitan society with strong diaspora ties
- Dual citizenship permitted in practice

### Well-known problems
- 2019 financial collapse; banking crisis and capital controls
- Weak passport (~42 visa-free, Henley 2026)
- Naturalisation effectively frozen; no path to citizenship
- Unreliable electricity, fuel, and public services
- Political deadlock and regional instability (FH 39/100 Partly Free)
