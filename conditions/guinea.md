## Overview
Guinea (Guinea-Conakry) is a West African coastal nation rich in mineral resources (bauxite, gold) with a population of ~14 million. Immigration is governed by the Guinean Civil Code, with limited formal residency pathways. Naturalization requires 5 years of residence. The passport provides visa‑free access to 28 destinations. ^[https://data.worldbank.org/country/GN]

## Per-Program Detail

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Permis de travail / Carte de sejour
- **Conditions**: Binding job offer from Guinean employer; employer sponsors work permit.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–2 years, tied to contract; renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years legal residence for naturalization. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_nationality_law]
- **Official source**: No comprehensive online portal; Direction Nationale de la Police des Etrangers.
- **Community verdict**: No reliable data (very few expat reports; bureaucratic friction expected).

### student_residency
- **Official name**: Visa d'etude
- **Conditions**: Enrollment in recognized Guinean institution; proof of financial means; health insurance.
- **Duration / renewal**: Course duration, renewable annually.
- **Path to citizenship**: Residence time counts toward 5‑year naturalization clock.
- **Official source**: Guinean embassies / consulates.
- **Community verdict**: No reliable data.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: Regroupement familial
- **Conditions**: Sponsor with valid permit; proof of family relationship and accommodation.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1–2 years, aligned with sponsor.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years residence.
- **Official source**: Guinean Civil Code provisions.
- **Community verdict**: No reliable data.

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: 5 years of legal residence for naturalization. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinean_nationality_law]
- **Language**: French proficiency expected (not formally examined for most).
- **Dual citizenship**: Not generally permitted (renunciation typically required).
- **Processing**: Discretionary, by presidential decree.

## Tax
- **Standard rates**: Personal income tax progressive. Corporate tax: 25%.
- **Remote workers**: No special regime; foreign‑source income may not be taxed if remitted, but enforcement is inconsistent.
- **General note**: Informal tax administration; few digital‑nomad‑specific rules.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No dedicated digital‑nomad visa. Internet is developing (4G in Conakry, limited elsewhere). Coworking is scarce. Guinea is not a recognized remote‑work destination.

## Yacht / Maritime
Atlantic coastline (~320 km) with the port of Conakry. Yacht tourism is minimal; no dedicated yacht‑registration flag. Maritime infrastructure is basic.

## Notes for RU Citizens
Russian citizens require a visa from Guinean embassies. No special programs. Passport is weak (28 destinations). Diplomatic relations cordial but thin.

## Community Pulse
Virtually no expat immigration reports. Guinea is not on nomad, retiree, or investor‑immigration forums. Expect bureaucratic paper‑driven procedures. Guinea-Bissau is regionally discussed as alternative.

## Country Card

### General description
Guinea is a mineral‑rich West African coastal nation with a predominantly Muslim population, French as the official language, and an economy anchored in bauxite mining. It has limited immigration infrastructure, no dedicated nomad or investor visas, and a weak passport. Naturalization is possible after 5 years of residence but is discretionary.

### Well-known strong points
- Rich mineral resources (world's largest bauxite reserves)
- Francophone gateway to West Africa
- Relatively short naturalization period (5 years)
- Atlantic coastline with port access

### Well-known problems
- Weak rule of law (WJP rank 119/142, FH 30/100)
- Lack of formal residency programs beyond work/study
- Underdeveloped infrastructure and bureaucracy
- Dual citizenship not generally permitted; passport weak (28 destinations)

- **Last monitored**: 2026-11-02
