## Overview
Barbados is a high-income Caribbean nation with an established digital nomad programme, the Barbados Welcome Stamp. It also offers standard work permits and permanent residence routes. The Welcome Stamp allows remote workers to live in Barbados for 12 months, renewable indefinitely. Barbados has no income tax on foreign-source income for non-residents; territorial system applies. Citizenship via naturalization requires long-term residence. ^[https://www.investbarbados.org/welcome-stamp]

## Per-Program Detail

### digital_nomad_visa
- **Official name**: 12-Month Barbados Welcome Stamp
- **Conditions**: Valid passport; annual income ≥ US$50,000 (or means to support self + dependents); work must be for entities outside Barbados; valid health insurance. ^[https://barbadoswelcomestamp.bb/applynow]
- **Duration / renewal**: 12 months; renewable indefinitely (subject to re-application and fee payment). Renewals get 25% discount on visa fee.
- **Fees**: Individual US$2,000; Family bundle US$3,000 (non-refundable). Renewal: $1,500 individual / $2,250 family.
- **Path to citizenship**: Welcome Stamp does not count toward permanent residence or citizenship. Years on Welcome Stamp are NOT resident years for naturalization.
- **Official source**: https://barbadoswelcomestamp.bb/applynow
- **Community verdict**: Works well (fast 7-day approvals; genuine no-local-tax setup; established since 2020; popular with North American/UK remote workers).

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Special Entry Permit / Independent Means Residence
- **Conditions**: Proof of sufficient independent means or passive income. Property ownership in Barbados (purchased residence) significantly aids application. Exact minimum income not statutorily fixed — assessed case-by-case. ^[customary threshold ~US$50,000/yr]
- **Duration / renewal**: Indefinite — not annually renewed.
- **Path to citizenship**: Must convert to permanent residence / immigrant status first. Naturalization typically requires 5+ years continuous residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.bb
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (discretionary — no fixed income threshold published; property ownership effectively required; slow processing).

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Barbados Work Permit
- **Conditions**: Binding job offer from Barbadian employer; employer must demonstrate no suitable local candidate; labour market test applies.
- **Duration / renewal**: Annual, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Counts toward residence years. After 5+ years continuous legal residence, permanent residence possible → naturalization.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.bb
- **Community verdict**: Works well (small market; most expat workers are in finance, law, tech; employer-driven).

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Special Entry Permit (Retiree) / Immigrant Status
- **Conditions**: Proof of pension or sufficient foreign-source income to support self. Property purchase in Barbados strongly recommended. Health insurance required. No minimum dollar amount published — assessed case-by-case.
- **Duration / renewal**: Indefinite; no annual renewal.
- **Path to citizenship**: After 5+ years, apply for permanent residence → naturalization.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.bb
- **Community verdict**: Works well (popular with UK/Canadian retirees; no fixed income number aids flexibility but also creates uncertainty; property ownership is de facto requirement).

## Citizenship
- **By naturalization**: Available after 5–7 years of continuous legal residence; application to the Minister. Must demonstrate integration, good character, English proficiency (no formal exam — English is the official language).
- **By marriage**: Spouse of Barbadian citizen after 3 years of marriage.
- **Dual citizenship**: Fully recognized since 2022 (Constitutional amendment).

## Tax
- Territorial tax system: tax only on income sourced within Barbados.
- No capital gains tax. No inheritance tax. No wealth tax.
- Personal income tax: 12.5% on first BBD 50,000; 28.5% on remainder.
- Welcome Stamp visa holders: no Barbados income tax liability (work is foreign-source).

## IT / Digital Nomad
Welcome Stamp is the dedicated programme. 12-month renewable visa. $50K income threshold; $2,000 individual / $3,000 family fee. Zero local tax. Fast approval (~7 business days). Schools accessible for children. ^[https://barbadoswelcomestamp.bb/applynow]

## Yacht / Maritime
Barbados has an international ship registry. Yacht registration possible. Full-service marinas available.

## Notes for RU citizens
- Russian nationals eligible for Welcome Stamp (no restrictions). Must apply from abroad.
- Barbados has no Russian embassy — consular services via UK or other nearby missions.
- No path to citizenship via Welcome Stamp — only via long-term work permit/permanent residence routes.

## Community pulse
- Welcome Stamp widely praised — easy application, fast turnaround, genuine tax benefit.
- Permanent residence/Special Entry Permit: discretionary, property-focused; slower processing.
- Overall: expat-friendly, English-speaking, high quality of life.

## Country Card

### General description
Barbados is a high-income eastern Caribbean island nation and a stable parliamentary democracy. It has a service-based economy dominated by tourism, international business, and financial services. Barbados is notable among Caribbean jurisdictions for offering both a well-regarded digital nomad programme (the 12-Month Welcome Stamp, $50K income threshold) and a long-established territorial tax regime with no tax on foreign-source income. It is English-speaking, has high Human Development Index scores for the region, and offers a respected passport with strong visa-free access (~155 destinations). Citizenship requires 5–7 years of residence.

### Well-known strong points
- Welcome Stamp: one of the Caribbean's best digital nomad programmes (7-day approval, zero local tax)
- Strong passport: ~155 visa-free destinations including UK, Schengen, Singapore
- English-speaking with British common-law legal system
- High Freedom House score (94/100 — Free) and strong rule of law (WJP 0.66)
- Territorial tax: no tax on foreign-source income; no capital gains, inheritance, or wealth tax
- Stable democracy with high quality of life; good healthcare by Caribbean standards

### Well-known problems
- Very high cost of living (Numbeo index 72.8); among the most expensive in the Caribbean
- Welcome Stamp does not count toward permanent residence or citizenship
- Permanent residence/Special Entry Permit is discretionary — no fixed income threshold published
- Small economy; limited job market outside tourism and financial services
- Long naturalization timeline (5–7 years) compared to CBI jurisdictions
- Hurricane risk (though historically less exposed than northern Caribbean)
