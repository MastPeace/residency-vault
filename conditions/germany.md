## Overview
Germany is the largest EU economy with one of the world's strongest passports. Its immigration system was reformed significantly through the Skilled Immigration Act (Fachkräfteeinwanderungsgesetz, 2020/2023), introducing the Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte, June 2024), and the 2024 citizenship reform that allows dual citizenship and reduces the residence requirement to 5 years (the 3-year fast-track was scrapped in 2026). Germany has no dedicated digital nomad visa; remote workers use the EU Blue Card, Freelancer visa, or Opportunity Card routes. ^[https://msingermany.com/blog/eu-blue-card-germany-2026]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Not available.
- **Conditions**: Germany has no dedicated investor visa. The Self-Employment Permit (§21 AufenthG) can serve business investors if the project has economic interest or regional need, sufficient funding, and positive prognosis from relevant authorities. No minimum investment threshold by law; assessed case-by-case.
- **Duration / renewal**: Up to 3 years.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years; B1 German; dual permitted.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (no statutory minimum but subjective assessment; not comparable to Portugal/Greece Golden Visas).

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Not available.
- **Conditions**: Germany does not offer a passive income residence route for non-EU nationals. The Freelancer Visa (Freiberufler, §21(5) AufenthG) in cities like Berlin is work-based (requires active freelance work for German clients), not passive-income-based.
- **Duration / renewal**: N/A.
- **Path to citizenship**: N/A.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (lack of passive income route is a gap; freelancer visa is not equivalent).

### digital_nomad_visa
- **Official name**: Not available.
- **Conditions**: No dedicated DN visa. Options: Freelancer Visa (Berlin, limited professions — artists, writers, IT consultants, etc.) requires active freelance work, German clients preferred. Self-Employment Permit for business setup. The Opportunity Card allows job search but not ongoing remote work. ^[https://handbookgermany.de/en/opportunity-card]
- **Duration / renewal**: N/A.
- **Path to citizenship**: N/A.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (no DN visa is a competitive gap; freelancer visa is city-specific and restrictive).

### highly_skilled_employment
- **Official name**: Blaue Karte EU (§18g AufenthG) — EU Blue Card
- **Conditions**: University degree OR for IT specialists: 3+ years professional experience within last 7 years (no degree required). Binding job offer ≥ 6 months. Salary thresholds 2026: €50,700/yr general (≈€4,225/mo); €45,934/yr for shortage occupations (IT, engineering, medicine, etc.). ^[https://msingermany.com/blog/eu-blue-card-germany-2026]
- **Duration / renewal**: Up to 4 years (contract duration + 3 months). Settlement permit (PR): 21 months with B1 German, 27 months with A1.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years; B1 German; dual permitted since 2024.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Works well (fast PR timeline, spouse joins without German + immediate work rights; best route into Germany).

### startup_entrepreneur
- **Official name**: Selbstständigkeit (§21 AufenthG) — Self-Employment Permit
- **Conditions**: Viable business plan; economic interest or regional need; sufficient financing (no statutory minimum); positive prognosis from IHK (Chamber of Commerce) and relevant authorities. Startup founders often use this route.
- **Duration / renewal**: Up to 3 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years; B1 German; dual permitted.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (subjective approval; IHK/authority assessment varies by region; Berlin more startup-friendly than other Länder).

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Fachkraft mit Berufsausbildung (§18a) / Fachkraft mit akademischer Ausbildung (§18b)
- **Conditions**: Recognized qualification (university degree or vocational training) + binding job offer. No labor market test (abolished 2023). For non-regulated professions: recognition of foreign qualification required.
- **Duration / renewal**: Contract duration; permanent residence after 36 months (33 with B1, 21 with B1 on Blue Card).
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years; B1 German; dual permitted.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Works well (labor market test abolished; qualification recognition is main bottleneck).

### student_residency
- **Official name**: Aufenthaltserlaubnis zum Studium (§16b)
- **Conditions**: University enrollment; blocked account with €11,208/yr (2026); health insurance. Part-time work permitted (140 full days or 280 half days per year).
- **Duration / renewal**: Course duration, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years (student years count); job-seeker permit (18 months) after graduation.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: Familiennachzug (§27–36 AufenthG)
- **Conditions**: Sponsor with valid permit; sufficient income and accommodation; A1 German for spouse (EXEMPT for EU Blue Card holders, skilled workers). Blue Card spouses: no German required + immediate full work rights.
- **Duration / renewal**: Aligned with sponsor.
- **Path to citizenship**: 5 years; B1 German; dual permitted.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Works well (Blue Card spouse exemption is major advantage).

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Not available.
- **Conditions**: Germany has no retirement visa. Non-EU retirees cannot obtain residence solely on pension income unless qualifying through another route (family reunification, prior residence converted to PR, EU long-term residence).
- **Duration / renewal**: N/A.
- **Path to citizenship**: N/A.
- **Official source**: https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (no retirement route; Germany is not a retirement destination for non-EU nationals).

### citizenship_by_investment
Not available. Germany does not offer direct citizenship by investment.

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: 5 years legal residence (reduced from 8 in 2024); 3-year fast-track scrapped in 2026. ^[https://msingermany.com/blog/german-citizenship-2026-explained]
- **Language**: B1 German (CEFR).
- **Naturalization test**: Einbürgerungstest (33 questions on German law, history, culture).
- **Dual citizenship**: Permitted since June 2024 (no renunciation required). ^[https://migrando.de/en/blog/naturalization/doppelte-staatsbuergerschaft]
- **Declaration of commitment**: To the free democratic constitutional order (freiheitlich-demokratische Grundordnung).
- **Processing**: 6–18 months, varies by municipality.
- **Political pushback (2026)**: CDU/CSU and Junge Union demand rollback of the "Ampel" citizenship law — return to 8-year residence, stricter values check, and abolition of dual citizenship. Coalition agreement currently protects key provisions; 310,000 naturalizations in 2025. ^[https://www.welt.de/themen/staatsbuergerschaft] *Rumor/watching* — no legislative action yet.

## Tax
- **Standard rates**: Progressive 14%–45% (2026 brackets: 0% up to €12,096; 14%–42% from €12,097 to €277,826; 45% above). Solidarity surcharge: 5.5% on tax amount (phased out for most). Church tax: 8–9% (optional).
- **Social security**: ~20% employee share (pension 9.3%, health 7.3%+, unemployment 1.3%, nursing care 1.8–2.3%).
- **No special impatriate/flat-tax regime**. Germany is a high-tax jurisdiction with extensive double taxation treaties.
- **Freelancer/self-employed**: Subject to same progressive rates; VAT (Umsatzsteuer) 19%.

## IT / Digital Nomad
Germany has no digital nomad visa. IT professionals should use the EU Blue Card (IT specialists qualify WITHOUT a degree via 3+ years experience — a unique German advantage). The Freelancer Visa in Berlin is city-specific and limited to certain liberal professions (IT consultants, software developers qualify in some cases). Berlin, Munich, and Hamburg are major tech hubs. The Opportunity Card allows 12-month job search but not ongoing remote work for foreign employers.

## Yacht / Maritime
Germany has North and Baltic Sea coastlines. German flag yacht registration available. Major marinas: Kiel, Rostock, Hamburg. Yacht/boat licensing follows German standards (SBF See, SKS, etc.), recognized internationally. Limited yachting scene compared to Mediterranean countries.

## Notes for RU Citizens
Russian citizens require a Schengen visa. German consulates in Russia have reduced operations. The EU Blue Card and Skilled Worker routes are open but Russian applicants face enhanced scrutiny and delays. The Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) is accessible but points-based system may disadvantage those without German language. Germany has a large Russian-speaking diaspora (~3M), easing integration. Dual citizenship permitted since 2024 is a major advantage. No dedicated passive income or retiree route — employment/qualification-based immigration only.

## Community Pulse
Germany's immigration system is overwhelmingly employment-oriented: the EU Blue Card is the gold standard with fast PR (21 months at B1) and excellent family rights. The citizenship reform (5 years, dual OK) is transformative. The lack of passive-income, digital-nomad, and retirement visas is a competitive gap vs. Portugal/Spain/Italy. The 3-year fast-track citizenship scrapping (2026) was a regression.

**2026 operational changes**: EES biometric system active on all Schengen external borders since April 10, 2026 (4 fingerprints + photo). Family reunification partially suspended for subsidiary protection holders from July 1, 2026. Bürgergeld replaced by Neue Grundsicherung. ^[https://msingermany.com/blog/germany-immigration-rules-2026-changes] Ausländerbehörde (foreigners' office) processing times are a chronic pain point — varies enormously by city (Berlin worst, smaller cities faster).

## Country Card

### General description
Germany is the EU's largest economy and most populous member state, with a highly developed industrial base (automotive, engineering, chemicals) and a strong export orientation. Its immigration system is overwhelmingly employment-oriented, anchored by the EU Blue Card (IT specialists qualify without a degree via 3+ years experience) and the Skilled Worker Visa. The 2024 citizenship reform (5 years, dual citizenship permitted) was transformative, though the 3-year fast-track was scrapped in 2026. Germany has no passive-income, digital nomad, or retirement visa — immigration runs almost exclusively through employment.

### Well-known strong points
- Largest EU economy with strong job market and high salaries (~€2,860/mo net)
- EU Blue Card: IT specialists qualify without a degree (3+ years experience — unique in EU)
- Fast permanent residence: 21 months with B1 German on Blue Card
- Citizenship in 5 years with dual citizenship permitted (since 2024)
- Strong Rule of Law (WJP 0.83, rank 5/142) and political stability
- Excellent public infrastructure, healthcare, and education

### Well-known problems
- No digital nomad visa, passive-income route, or retirement visa
- High tax and social-security burden (effective rate ~40%+ for mid/high earners)
- Ausländerbehörde (foreigners' office) processing times vary enormously — Berlin worst
- Housing crisis in major cities (Berlin, Munich, Hamburg)
- B1 German language requirement for citizenship; integration course required
- Political pushback (CDU/CSU) to roll back citizenship reform creates uncertainty
