## Overview
Germany is the largest EU economy with one of the world's strongest passports. Its immigration system was reformed significantly with the Skilled Immigration Act (2020/2023) and the Opportunity Card (2024/2025). Citizenship was liberalized in 2024 to allow dual citizenship and reduce residence requirement to 5 years (3 for exceptional integration). ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

## Residence
Key residence routes for non-EU nationals:

**EU Blue Card**: for university graduates with a binding job offer and salary ≥ €43,800 (2026; €39,682 for shortage occupations). Valid for up to 4 years. After 27 months (21 with B1 German), eligible for permanent residence. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

**Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)**: introduced June 2024. Allows skilled non-EU workers to enter Germany without a job offer and search for employment for up to 12 months. Points-based: education, experience, age, German language, prior stays in Germany. Requires proof of financial means (~€1,091/month). As of May 2026, applications are routed through the Foreign Office's Consular Services Portal for digital processing. ^[raw/articles/monitor-snapshot-2026-09-13.json]

**Skilled Worker Visa (Fachkraft)**: for qualified professionals with recognized qualifications and a job offer. Labor market test abolished.

## Citizenship
- 5 years of legal residence (3 years with exceptional integration + C1 German)
- German language proficiency (B1)
- Citizenship test (naturalization test)
- Dual citizenship permitted (since June 2024)
- Declaration of commitment to the German constitutional order

## Tax
Progressive income tax: 14%–45% (solidarity surcharge adds 5.5% on the tax amount). Social security contributions ~20% (split employer/employee). Germany has double taxation agreements with most countries.

## IT / Digital Nomad
Germany does not offer a specific [[digital-nomad]] visa. Remote workers can use:
- The Opportunity Card (job search, not remote work)
- Freelancer visa (for certain liberal professions in specific cities like Berlin)
- The [[blue-card]] (requires German employer)
Germany is a major tech hub: Berlin, Munich, and Hamburg have strong startup scenes.

## Yacht / Maritime
Germany has coastlines on the North and Baltic Seas. Yacht registration under the German flag is available. Major marinas on the Baltic coast (Kiel, Rostock). Yacht and boat licensing follows German standards (SBF See, SKS, etc.) recognized internationally.

## Notes for RU citizens
Russian citizens require a visa for Germany (Schengen). The German consulates in Russia may have reduced operations. The Opportunity Card and Blue Card routes are open in principle but applications from Russia face enhanced scrutiny and delays. Germany has a large Russian-speaking diaspora (~3M people), which eases cultural integration.
