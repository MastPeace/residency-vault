## Overview
The Bahamas has no citizenship-by-investment programme and no digital nomad visa. Access is through work permits (annual), permanent residence (economic or long-term work), or property ownership. The country has a territorial tax system with no personal income tax, no capital gains tax, and no inheritance tax. Naturalization requires 10 years of permanent residence with at least 6 years of physical presence. ^[https://www.immigration.gov.bs/applying-to-stay/applying-for-citizenship]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Permanent Residence (Economic)
- **Conditions**: Minimum US$1,000,000 investment in Bahamian real estate OR Zero Coupon Bonds from the Central Bank. Asset must be held 10 years minimum. ^[https://www.immigration.gov.bs/residence/permanent-residence]
- **Duration / renewal**: Permanent — issued for life (unless revoked). Also possible via: spouse of Bahamian (after 5 years marriage), 20+ years on work permits, 10+ years special categories (police, teachers, nurses, doctors, ministers).
- **Path to citizenship**: After 10 years of holding permanent residence (including 12 months immediately before application) + 6 years physical presence.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.bs/residence/permanent-residence
- **Community verdict**: Works well for high-net-worth individuals ($1,000,000+ buy-in); discretionary processing; property ownership is the practical route.

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Annual Residence Permit (non-work)
- **Conditions**: Proof of independent financial means; property ownership (or long-term lease) in Bahamas preferred. Not statutorily fixed dollar amount — assessed case-by-case.
- **Duration / renewal**: Annual, renewable. Processing fee: $200 + $10 stamp. Processing 8–12 weeks.
- **Path to citizenship**: Does NOT count directly toward permanent residence. Must convert to permanent residence first.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.bs
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (discretionary; property ownership effectively required; slow processing 8–12 weeks).

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Annual Work Permit
- **Conditions**: Letter of request to Director of Immigration; completed Form I with $10 stamp; Labour Certificate from Dept of Labour; 3 consecutive days of newspaper vacancy ads; 2 employer references; medical certificate (≤30 days old); police certificate (≤6 months, covering 5 years). ^[https://www.immigration.gov.bs/residence/annual-work-permit]
- **Duration / renewal**: Annual, renewable. Processing: 8–12 weeks. $200 non-refundable processing fee.
- **Path to citizenship**: After 20+ consecutive years on work permits → permanent residence; after 10 more years as permanent resident → citizenship.
- **Official source**: https://www.immigration.gov.bs/residence/annual-work-permit
- **Community verdict**: Works but slow (8–12 week processing; labour market test requires job ads; employer-driven).

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Annual Residence Permit (retiree) / Permanent Residence
- **Conditions**: Proof of pension or foreign-source passive income; property ownership or long-term lease in Bahamas. Homeowner route: purchasing a residence for BSD 1,500,000+ (~US$1,500,000) gets "speedy consideration" for permanent residence. ^[https://www.immigration.gov.bs/residence/permanent-residence]
- **Duration / renewal**: Annual (residence permit) or permanent (if meeting $1,500,000 property threshold).
- **Path to citizenship**: After permanent residence: 10 years + 6 years physical presence.
- **Official source**: https://mofa.gov.bs/applying-for-permanent-residence
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (dual-track: wealthy retirees buying $1,500,000+ homes get fast-track permanent residence; others on annual permits face slow, discretionary process).

## Citizenship
- **By naturalization**: 10 years as legal permanent resident including 12 months immediately before application, PLUS at least 6 years physical presence in preceding 12-month period's prior years. Good character, English proficiency (no formal exam), intent to reside. ^[https://www.immigration.gov.bs/applying-to-stay/applying-for-citizenship]
- **By marriage**: Female spouse of Bahamian citizen may register for citizenship (male spouse must naturalize).
- **Dual citizenship**: NOT recognized by Bahamas. Naturalizing citizens must renounce prior nationality.

## Tax
- No personal income tax. No corporate income tax. No capital gains tax. No inheritance tax. No wealth tax.
- Revenue from VAT (10%), customs duties, stamp duties, property tax.
- No double-taxation agreements; tax residency not heavily policed.

## IT / Digital Nomad
Not available. No dedicated digital nomad visa. Remote workers may enter as tourists and work remotely for foreign employers (90-day tourist visa extendable to 8 months). No legal framework for long-term digital nomad residence.

## Yacht / Maritime
The Bahamas is a major yacht and maritime hub — one of the world's largest ship registries (Bahamas Maritime Authority). Yacht registration, crew visas, and cruising permits all well-established. No special yacht-residence programme, but crews are accommodated under maritime-specific work permit channels.

## Notes for RU citizens
- Russian nationals require a visa to enter The Bahamas. No visa-free access.
- Annual residence permits available but discretionary. Property purchase strongly recommended.
- No dual citizenship means naturalization requires renouncing Russian citizenship — a significant barrier.

## Community pulse
- High barrier to entry for non-millionaires. Work permit path is slow (20 years to permanent residence). Citizenship path is among the longest in the region (20+ years total).
- Bahamas is a tax haven — popular with those who have substantial offshore wealth and don't need local citizenship.
- Expats generally satisfied with quality of life but frustrated with immigration bureaucracy.

## Country Card

### General description
The Bahamas is an archipelagic nation of over 700 islands stretching from near Florida to the Caribbean. It is a high-income country with a GDP per capita among the highest in the Americas, driven by tourism (over 60% of GDP) and offshore financial services. The Bahamas combines zero personal income tax with a strong passport (~153 visa-free destinations), making it attractive to high-net-worth individuals. However, residency is reserved for the wealthy: permanent residence requires $1,000,000+ in real estate investment, and the path to citizenship is among the longest in the region at 20+ years. Dual citizenship is not recognized, requiring renunciation of prior nationality.

### Well-known strong points
- Zero personal income tax, zero capital gains tax, zero inheritance tax
- Strong passport: ~153 visa-free destinations including Schengen, UK, Canada
- High GDP per capita (~$43,000 PPP) and developed infrastructure
- English-speaking with British common-law traditions
- World-class tourism and maritime hub; major yachting destination
- Politically stable democracy (FH 91/100, Free)

### Well-known problems
- One of the most expensive countries in the Caribbean (Numbeo index 85.1)
- Permanent residence requires $1,000,000+ investment; no accessible mid-tier residency
- Path to citizenship is extremely long: 20+ years total
- Dual citizenship NOT recognized — naturalization requires renouncing prior nationality
- No digital nomad visa; no legal framework for remote workers beyond tourist entry
- Annual residence permits are discretionary with 8–12 week processing
