## Overview
The Turks and Caicos Islands (TCI) is a British Overseas Territory and a luxury tourism destination. It imposes **no direct taxes** (no income, capital gains, corporate, or inheritance tax). There is no citizenship-by-investment programme; residence is available via work permits, a Permanent Residence Certificate (including an investment route), and, ultimately, naturalisation as a British Overseas Territories Citizen. ^[https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/turks-caicosislands-residency.pdf]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Permanent Residence Certificate (PRC)
- **Conditions**: TCI offers a Permanent Residence Certificate to persons of independent means / investors, historically requiring an investment (commonly cited ~US$300,000 in real estate, with a higher-value "fast-track" route). Conditions are set by the TCI government and subject to change; verify current thresholds. ^[https://www.gov.tc/]
- **Duration / renewal**: Indefinite (some classes permit work).
- **Path to citizenship**: BOTC naturalisation after ~5 years' residence.
- **Official source**: https://www.gov.tc/
- **Community verdict**: No reliable data (low volume; thresholds not consistently published).

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Work permit
- **Conditions**: Employer-sponsored; most expatriates on Providenciales hold work permits in tourism/finance. ^[https://www.gov.tc/]
- **Duration / renewal**: Tied to employment; renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Long residence → PRC → BOTC naturalisation.
- **Official source**: https://www.gov.tc/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (accessible in tourism; long clock to PR).

## Citizenship
Citizens are British Overseas Territories Citizens (BOTC) and, since 2002, generally also full British citizens (British passport). Naturalisation as BOTC is possible after ~5 years' residence; full "Belonger status" (right to vote/own land) is separate and takes longer. Dual citizenship is permitted. ^[https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/british-overseas-territories-citizens/british-overseas-territories-citizens-accessible-version]

## Tax
**0% income tax, 0% capital gains, 0% corporate, 0% inheritance** — no direct taxation of any kind. Government revenue comes from import duties and property-related fees (stamp duty on transfers). No tax residency framework and no tax filing. ^[https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issue-focus/aeoi/turks-caicosislands-residency.pdf]

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital-nomad visa. Remote workers would need a work permit or a PRC (investment route); there is no lightweight remote-work certificate.

## Yacht / Maritime
TCI is a Caribbean yachting and bareboat-charter destination (Providenciales, Grace Bay). It operates within the UK Red Ensign flag framework; marinas are concentrated around Providenciales.

## Notes for RU Citizens
No CBI or fast track. The PRC investment route is the realistic path for self-funded residence; work permits are tourism-sector-oriented. 0% tax is the main draw, but the territory is small and remote.

## Community Pulse
Turks and Caicos appears in expat forums mainly for luxury tourism and high-end retirement. The zero-tax regime and British citizenship route are praised; the opaque, changeable PRC investment thresholds and the small job market are the main complaints.

## Country Card

### General description
The Turks and Caicos Islands is a small, affluent British Overseas Territory southeast of the Bahamas, built on luxury beach tourism (Grace Bay) and offshore finance. It offers a zero-direct-tax environment and a British citizenship route, but its residency options are limited and its PRC investment thresholds are not consistently published.

### Well-known strong points
- Zero direct taxation (income, capital gains, corporate, inheritance)
- British legal system and BOTC/British citizenship route
- High-end tourism economy; safe and stable
- English-speaking

### Well-known problems
- No digital-nomad or passive-income visa
- Opaque, changeable PRC investment thresholds
- Very high cost of living (import-dependent)
- Small, tourism-concentrated job market; hurricane exposure
