## Overview
Austria is a wealthy EU/Schengen country with a strong passport and competitive [[blue-card]] programme. It offers multiple work-based residence permits including the Red-White-Red Card (points-based) and the EU Blue Card. Citizenship is among the most demanding in Europe: 10 years of legal residence, German proficiency (B2), and strict integration requirements. ^[https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/eu-blue-card]

## Residence
Key residence permits for non-EU nationals:

**EU Blue Card**: ^[https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/eu-blue-card]
- University degree (min. 3 years), or for IT professionals: 3+ years of comparable experience acquired in the last 7 years
- Binding job offer ≥ 6 months
- **Salary threshold (2026)**: ≥ €55,678 gross annual (average gross annual income of full-time employees)
- Labor market test applies (AMS checks for equally qualified registered jobseekers)
- Valid: 24 months
- After 2 years on Blue Card + 21 months of employment: eligible for Red-White-Red Card Plus (free labor market access)

**Red-White-Red Card**: points-based system for qualified workers in shortage occupations (60+ specialties). Points for qualifications, work experience, language, age. Valid: 24 months.

**Red-White-Red Card Plus**: free access to labor market. Available after 2 years on RWR Card or Blue Card, or for family members.

**Settlement Permit – Gainful Employment Excepted**: for financially independent individuals (no work). Very limited quotas.

## Citizenship
- 10 years of legal continuous residence (minimum)
- B2 German language proficiency
- Comprehensive integration test
- Proof of stable income and accommodation
- **Renunciation of previous citizenship generally required** (Austria does not permit dual citizenship except by special approval)
- High discretion: even meeting all formal requirements, approval is not guaranteed

## Tax
Progressive income tax: 0%–55% (top rate from €1M/year). Standard top rate of 50% applies above €90,000. Social security contributions: ~18% employee share. No wealth tax. Austria has a wide double taxation treaty network.

## IT / Digital Nomad
Austria does not offer a dedicated [[digital-nomad]] visa. IT professionals primarily use the [[blue-card]] route (IT professionals can qualify via work experience without a degree). Vienna has a growing startup scene and ranks high for quality of life but is expensive. Some remote workers operate via the Red-White-Red Card route if they meet points criteria.

## Yacht / Maritime
Not applicable. Austria is landlocked. Limited recreational boating on lakes with domestic licensing.

## Notes for RU citizens
Austrian consulates in Russia may have reduced services. Residence permit applications are challenging: high salary thresholds, labor market tests, and strict quotas. The language requirement (B2 German for citizenship) is among the highest in Europe. Austria does NOT historically have a large Russian-speaking community. The non-immigration-friendly climate (no dual citizenship, no digital nomad visa) makes Austria one of the less accessible destinations for Russian nationals.
