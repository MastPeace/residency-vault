## Overview
Austria is a wealthy EU/Schengen country with a strong passport and a points-based immigration system. The main residence permits are the EU Blue Card and the Red-White-Red Card (Rot-Weiß-Rot Karte, RWR). Citizenship is among the most demanding in Europe: 10 years of continuous legal residence, B2 German, and renunciation of previous citizenship (generally required). Austria has no digital nomad visa and no dedicated investor residence route; it is an employment-qualification-focused system. ^[https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/eubluecard]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Not available.
- **Conditions**: Austria has no dedicated investor visa. Business self-employment (Red-White-Red Card for self-employed key workers) may qualify if the business creates jobs, brings capital investment, and passes AMS regional review. No statutory minimum investment; assessed case-by-case by the provincial governor.
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years (RWR Card for self-employed).
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years; B2 German; renunciation generally required.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (no clear pathway; limited to genuine high-impact business cases).

### passive_income_residency
- **Official name**: Niederlassungsbewilligung — ausgenommen Erwerbstätigkeit (Settlement Permit — Gainful Employment Excepted)
- **Conditions**: Proof of fixed and regular income sufficient to cover living costs without work; private health insurance; accommodation. Subject to very limited annual provincial quotas — often exhausted early. NO work permitted (including remote). ^[https://www.migration.gv.at/]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable. Permanent residence (Daueraufenthalt-EU) after 5 years.
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years; B2 German; renunciation generally required.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (quota system makes this effectively inaccessible; not a reliable route).

### digital_nomad_visa
- **Official name**: Not available.
- **Conditions**: No digital nomad visa. Remote workers cannot use the Settlement Permit (work prohibited). The RWR Card requires an Austrian employer. Freelancers may theoretically qualify for self-employment permit but face heavy administrative hurdles and quota limitations.
- **Duration / renewal**: N/A.
- **Path to citizenship**: N/A.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (no DN route; Austria is not nomad-friendly).

### highly_skilled_employment
- **Official name**: Blaue Karte EU (EU Blue Card)
- **Conditions**: University degree (min 3 years) OR for IT professionals: 3+ years comparable experience in last 7 years. Binding job offer ≥ 6 months. Salary ≥ €55,678/yr gross (2026, average gross annual income of full-time employees). Labor market test applies (AMS checks for equally qualified registered jobseekers). ^[https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/eubluecard]
- **Duration / renewal**: 24 months. After 2 years on Blue Card + 21 months employment: eligible for RWR Card Plus (free labor market access).
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years; B2 German; renunciation generally required.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (high salary threshold €55,678 vs Germany €50,700; labor market test is additional hurdle; small quota vs Germany).

### startup_entrepreneur
- **Official name**: Rot-Weiß-Rot Karte für Start-up Gründer (RWR Card — Start-up Founders)
- **Conditions**: Innovative product/service/process; minimum €50,000 share capital (with at least 50% held by founder); business plan; must score 50+ points in start-up criteria (product, IP, management experience, capital). ^[https://www.migration.gv.at/]
- **Duration / renewal**: 2 years, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years; B2 German; renunciation generally required.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (structured program but high entry bar: €50K capital + innovation test).

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Rot-Weiß-Rot Karte (Red-White-Red Card — points-based)
- **Conditions**: Points-based system: minimum 70 out of 100 points for "particularly highly qualified" (education, experience, language, age, research). Alternative: "skilled workers in shortage occupations" (60+ listed professions) with job offer + salary ≥ legally stipulated minimum. "Other key workers": job offer + labor market test + salary ≥ 1.5× ASVG maximum (≈€3,930/mo for under 30, €4,720/mo for 30+). ^[https://www.migrant.at/wp-content/uploads/RWR-EN-2026.pdf]
- **Duration / renewal**: 24 months. RWR Card Plus after 2 years (free labor market access).
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years; B2 German; renunciation generally required.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (complex multi-category system; points threshold demanding; but structured and predictable for qualified applicants).

### student_residency
- **Official name**: Aufenthaltsbewilligung — Student
- **Conditions**: Enrollment in Austrian university or other educational institution; financial means; health insurance; accommodation.
- **Duration / renewal**: Course duration, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: Student years count 50% toward 10-year clock. Job-seeker permit (RWR Card for graduates) after graduation.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Works well.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: Rot-Weiß-Rot Karte Plus / Familienzusammenführung
- **Conditions**: Sponsor with valid permit; sufficient income and accommodation; A1 German for spouse (must be completed before entry in most cases). Spouses of RWR Card / Blue Card holders get RWR Card Plus with free labor market access from day 1 (after 2 years of sponsor's residence, in some interpretations).
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 10 years; B2 German.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (A1 German pre-entry requirement is higher bar than most EU countries; processing delays).

### retired_residency
- **Official name**: Not available.
- **Conditions**: The Settlement Permit — Gainful Employment Excepted theoretically covers retirees, but annual quotas are minuscule and province-specific. Austria is not a realistic retirement destination for non-EU nationals.
- **Duration / renewal**: N/A.
- **Path to citizenship**: N/A.
- **Official source**: https://www.migration.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Problematic (effectively unavailable).

### citizenship_by_investment
- **Official name**: Exceptional grant under Art. 10(6) StbG
- **Conditions**: Discretionary grant by the federal government for "extraordinary merit" (scientific, economic, cultural, or sporting). Extremely rare; not a marketed program. No fixed investment amount.
- **Duration / renewal**: N/A (direct citizenship).
- **Path to citizenship**: Immediate.
- **Official source**: https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/
- **Community verdict**: Not applicable (not a real route).

## Citizenship
- **Residence requirement**: 10 years continuous legal residence (minimum). Reductions: 6 years for EEA citizens; 6 years for "sustainable personal integration" (B2 German, voluntary work); 4–6 years for spouses of Austrian citizens. ^[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_nationality_law]
- **Language**: B2 German (ÖSD, Goethe-Zertifikat).
- **Integration test**: Comprehensive test on Austrian history, constitution, and values.
- **Dual citizenship**: Generally NOT permitted (renunciation required). Exceptions: special approval by federal government; citizenship acquired by descent; cases where renunciation causes serious harm.
- **Processing**: 6–24 months; high discretion — meeting formal requirements does not guarantee approval.

## Tax
- **Standard rates**: Progressive 0%–55% (2026). 0% up to €13,308; 20% to €21,617; 30% to €36,300; 40% to €67,400; 48% to €1,000,000; 55% above €1,000,000.
- **Social security**: ~18% employee share.
- **No wealth tax**.
- **No special impatriate regime** comparable to Italy/Spain/Netherlands. Austria is a high-tax jurisdiction.

## IT / Digital Nomad
No digital nomad visa. IT professionals should use the EU Blue Card (IT specialists qualify via work experience without degree — 3+ years in last 7 years). The RWR Card covers "skilled workers in shortage occupations" which includes many IT roles. Vienna has a growing startup scene and ranks high for quality of life but is expensive. No freelancer visa comparable to Berlin.

## Yacht / Maritime
Not applicable. Austria is landlocked. Limited recreational boating on lakes with domestic licensing requirements.

## Notes for RU Citizens
Austrian consulates in Russia may have reduced operations. Residence permit applications are among the most challenging in the EU: high salary thresholds, labor market tests, strict quotas, and the demanding B2 German + renunciation citizenship package. Austria does not have a large Russian-speaking community. The non-immigration-friendly climate (no dual citizenship, no digital nomad visa, no passive-income route, quotas) makes Austria one of the least accessible EU destinations for Russian nationals.

## Community Pulse
Austria is consistently rated as one of the most difficult EU countries for non-EU immigration. The points-based RWR Card system is complex but predictable. The EU Blue Card salary threshold (€55,678) is the highest among EU Blue Card countries. Citizenship (10 years, B2 German, renunciation) is the most demanding package in Western Europe alongside Switzerland. The lack of passive-income, digital-nomad, and retiree routes limits options to employment-based immigration. Quality of life and public services are excellent once residency is obtained.
