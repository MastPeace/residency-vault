## Overview
Bolivia's immigration is managed by the Dirección General de Migración (DIGEMIG) under the Ministry of Government. Bolivia is not a major destination for investor or digital-nomad residency — there is no dedicated digital nomad visa and no formal investor residency program. The system centers on tourist entry (30 days/trip, up to 90 days/year) and a "Visa de Objeto Determinado" (Specific Purpose Visa) for work, study, or family purposes, followed by temporary stay regularization. Citizenship by naturalization requires 3 years of residence (1 year with a Bolivian spouse, Bolivian children, or property/industry). The Bolivian passport provides ~75 visa-free destinations. Bolivia operates a dual exchange rate (official vs. parallel) and uses the boliviano (BOB), indexed via the UFV. ^[https://consulados.cancilleria.gob.bo/washington-dc/visas]

## Per-Program Detail

### investment_residency
- **Official name**: Visa Múltiple (multiple-entry visa for investment and business activities)
- **Conditions**: Authorizes constant entry/exit for investment and business activities. Valid 1 year with stated term. No formal minimum investment published by DIGEMIG; business registration and economic activity documentation required. ^[https://bolivianembassy.ca/visas-english-version]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 3 years of residence (1 year with property/industry of value per nationality law).
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.gob.bo
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (thin documentation; process handled locally at DIGEMIG offices; limited English-language resources).

### passive_income_residency
Not available as a formal program. No dedicated rentista visa. Economic solvency (bank statement/credit card) is a supporting requirement for some Specific Purpose Visas, but there is no standalone passive-income residency category.

### digital_nomad_visa
Not available. Bolivia has no digital nomad visa. Remote workers enter as tourists (30 days/trip, up to 90 days/year) and work de facto, or obtain a Specific Purpose Visa.

### highly_skilled_employment
- **Official name**: Visa de Objeto Determinado (trabajo) + permanencia temporal
- **Conditions**: Employment contract with Bolivian employer; document accrediting the activity. Police/criminal record (16+). ^[https://consulados.cancilleria.gob.bo/washington-dc/visas]
- **Duration / renewal**: Visa valid up to 60 days for entry, enabling 1-year temporary stay before DIGEMIG, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 3 years residence (1 year with Bolivian spouse/children/property).
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.gob.bo
- **Community verdict**: Mixed (bureaucratic; local DIGEMIG office procedures vary by region).

### startup_entrepreneur
Subsumed under Visa Múltiple (business/investment). Business registration + activity documentation required.

### employment_residency
- **Official name**: Visa de Objeto Determinado (trabajo)
- **Conditions**: Employment contract with Bolivian employer; economic solvency for some categories; criminal record.
- **Duration / renewal**: 1-year temporary stay, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 3 years (1 year reduced).
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.gob.bo
- **Community verdict**: Mixed.

### student_residency
- **Official name**: Visa de Objeto Determinado (estudio)
- **Conditions**: Enrollment in Bolivian educational institution. Visa valid up to 60 days, enabling 1-year temporary stay renewable until study completion. ^[https://consulados.cancilleria.gob.bo/washington-dc/visas]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable to completion.
- **Path to citizenship**: 3 years (1 year reduced).
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.gob.bo
- **Community verdict**: Works well for enrolled students.

### family_reunification
- **Official name**: Visa de Objeto Determinado (familia)
- **Conditions**: Family tie to Bolivian citizen or resident. Exempt from economic solvency requirement. ^[https://consulados.cancilleria.gob.bo/washington-dc/visas]
- **Duration / renewal**: 1 year, renewable.
- **Path to citizenship**: 1 year of residence (Bolivian spouse or children).
- **Official source**: https://www.migracion.gob.bo
- **Community verdict**: Works well (fastest citizenship route via 1-year reduced period).

### retired_residency
Not available as a formal program. No dedicated retirement/pensionado residency category published by DIGEMIG. Retirees typically use the Specific Purpose Visa (family/other) or maintain tourist status.

### citizenship_by_investment
Not available.

## Citizenship
- **Naturalization**: 3 years of residence (general). Reduced to 1 year if the applicant has a Bolivian spouse or children, or owns immovable property, or introduces an industry/invention of value. ^[https://legal.un.org/legislativeseries/pdfs/chapters/book4/book4_bolivia.pdf]
- **Continuity**: Journeys abroad under 3 months for good reason do not interrupt residence but do not count toward the 3-year period.
- **Language**: Spanish (and/or indigenous languages) proficiency expected; no formal standardized exam widely documented.
- **Dual citizenship**: Historically Bolivia did not recognize dual nationality (nationality lost on acquiring foreign nationality). Current practice permits some dual nationality under the 2009 Constitution, but naturalized Bolivians may face renunciation expectations. **Confidence: low — verify with a Bolivian consulate.**
- **Maintenance**: A naturalized citizen who leaves Bolivia and resides abroad for 2+ years within the first 5 years may forfeit naturalization.

## Tax
- **Tax system**: Worldwide income for tax residents, but Bolivia's tax enforcement on foreign income is weak. Progressive rates on Bolivian-source income. ^[https://www.migracion.gob.bo]
- **Residency trigger**: Generally 183 days or center of vital interests.
- **Currency**: Boliviano (BOB); dual exchange rate (official ~6.9 BOB/USD vs. parallel) creates practical FX friction for foreign-income residents.
- **Wealth tax**: Yes (Impuesto a las Grandes Fortunas, large fortunes only).

## IT / Digital Nomad
- No digital nomad visa. Tourist entry (30 days/trip, up to 90/year) is the practical limit for short remote work.
- Internet: average; La Paz, Santa Cruz, and Cochabamba have reasonable connectivity but infrastructure lags regional peers.
- Bolivia is not a meaningful digital-nomad destination.

## Yacht / Maritime
Landlocked country. No maritime/yacht residency relevance.

## Notes for RU citizens
- Russian citizens: visa-free for tourism up to 90 days (Group I countries). ^[https://consulados.cancilleria.gob.bo/washington-dc/visas]
- Residency applications via DIGEMIG; documents must be apostilled and translated into Spanish.
- Banking: possible with Bolivian ID; dual exchange rate complicates USD transfers.
- Bolivia has no specific restrictions targeting Russian applicants, but the residency system is bureaucratic and not designed for remote/investor lifestyles.
- Bolivia is rarely chosen by RU citizens compared to Paraguay/Uruguay due to lack of a codified investor residency and 0% foreign-tax regime.

## Community pulse
- **Overall**: Bolivia is a low-demand residency destination. No investor visa, no digital nomad visa, weak tax advantages, and currency friction.
- **Processing**: Local DIGEMIG offices handle regularization; procedures vary by region and are not standardized online.
- **Rejection risk**: Low (few formal thresholds) but process is opaque and Spanish-only.
- **Confidence note**: Official English documentation is sparse; several program details could not be verified against a single authoritative source. Medium confidence.

## Country Card

### General description
Bolivia is a landlocked Andean country with one of the highest capital cities in the world (La Paz, 3,640m). It has the lowest GDP per capita in South America (~$10,500 PPP) and the weakest rule-of-law score among the countries in this set (WJP 0.38). Bolivia is not a meaningful destination for investor or digital-nomad residency — there is no dedicated investor visa, no digital nomad visa, and no formal passive-income/retirement residency. The citizenship path is short on paper (3 years, or 1 year for spouses/property owners), but the practical barriers (bureaucracy, dual exchange rate, limited English-language resources) are substantial.

### Well-known strong points
- Short citizenship timeline on paper: 3 years (1 year reduced with spouse/children/property)
- Very low cost of living (Numbeo index 24.0) — cheapest in this country set
- Dramatic geography: Andes highlands, Uyuni Salt Flats, Amazon basin, Lake Titicaca
- Visa-free tourist entry for most nationalities (90 days); Russian citizens visa-free
- Small but established expat communities in La Paz and Santa Cruz
- Low rejection risk for residency applications — few formal thresholds

### Well-known problems
- No dedicated investor visa, digital nomad visa, or retirement residency program
- Weakest rule of law in this country set (WJP 0.38, rank 131/142)
- Dual exchange rate (official ~6.9 BOB/USD vs. parallel) creates significant FX friction
- Tourist entry limited to 30 days/trip, 90 days/year — insufficient for remote workers
- Naturalized citizens who leave Bolivia for 2+ years within first 5 years may forfeit citizenship
- Opaque, Spanish-only bureaucracy; limited English-language resources; procedures vary by regional DIGEMIG office
